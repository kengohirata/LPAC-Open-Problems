# Complete-filter targets and a nonmonic-p ordinal no-go theorem

2026-11-18. Status: PROOF_RECONSTRUCTED. Another research agent independently reconstructed the proof, including the finite-arity generalization, and the coordinating agent read and checked the complete note. No external review or formal verification. This supplies actual M-targets with nonmonic p and then excludes one concrete family as a source of an ordinal equalizer obstruction. It is not a No.4 counterexample.

Write c=2^aleph_0. All target constructions and the no-go theorem are ZFC. An ordinal Y_kappa is assumed to belong to C only when that membership is needed; the no-go calculation itself uses its explicit two carriers and arrows.

## 1. Reduced powers by sufficiently complete filters belong to M

Let R belong to C and suppose both carriers of R are countable. Let F be a proper c^+-complete filter on a set I: intersections of at most c filter members remain in F. Form the pointwise reduced power

    U=(E(R))^I/F.

Equality is uniform within a row: two representatives are equal when the set of indices where the entire corresponding R_i-indexed functions agree belongs to F. All presheaf actions descend.

Then U belongs to M. Indeed, choose representatives for any finite family of elements in its two sorts. At row t these give a finite tuple of functions on the countable carriers R_0,R_1. There are at most c possible row types. Call a type positive if its occurrence set is not F-small, where an F-small set has complement in F. The union of all small type occurrence sets is small, by c^+-completeness. After restricting to its filter-large complement, every remaining type is positive.

Let P be the set of these positive types and put Q=coprod_(p in P) R. The set P is nonempty, since the filter is proper. Since C is colimit-closed, Q belongs to C. The row map taking the t-th copy of R identically into the copy labelled by its type induces

    E(Q) -> U.

On the discarded small set of rows, assign one fixed positive type arbitrarily; this makes the representative map globally defined without changing its germ.

This map is monic. If two functions on Q differ at a point in the p-th copy, they differ on every row of type p; that occurrence set is not small. The prescribed finite family factors through the map by construction. This proves M-membership. If R is both-finite-support, so is Q, and U belongs to M_bfs.

The same proof works for a larger fixed R whenever the filter is closed under intersections indexed by the number of possible finite row types. The countable case is enough below.

This is more general than a tail quotient. For example the cone filter on [Gamma]^(<=c), generated by the sets of fragments containing a specified <=c-sized subset of Gamma, is proper and c^+-complete. A compatible partial-model construction satisfying every <=c diagram fragment in E(R) would therefore produce an actual map into an M-target of the form above. This observation does not assert that the required partial models exist for an ordinal source.

## 2. A countable characterful C-template with nonmonic p on its dual

Let

    B=omega^3,
    A={x in B : at least two coordinates of x are equal},
    R_0=B,
    R_1=B amalgamated_over_A B.

Use the diagonal M_1-actions and let p_R,q_R:B->R_1 be the two pushout legs. The subset A is M_1-stable. Both carriers are countable and finite-support.

Every equivariant map omega^3->omega is a coordinate projection. To see this, evaluate at (0,1,2): equivariance forces the value to be one of these three entries, and every other tuple is its image under a transformation of omega. The three projections have pairwise distinct restrictions to A. Consequently

    H(R_1) = {(pi_i,pi_i): i=1,2,3},

and H(p_R) is bijective. Thus R belongs to C_bfs. Also H(q_R) agrees with H(p_R), so the induced character dynamics is the identity.

Neither pushout leg is onto: an injective triple in the other copy is absent from its image. Therefore E(p_R) is not monic. The two legs jointly cover R_1, however, so

    (E(p_R),E(q_R)):E(R)_1 -> E(R)_0 times E(R)_0

is monic. Both properties persist in the reduced power U from Section 1. Nonmonicity is witnessed by two constant-row functions differing only outside p_R(B), while joint monicity uses the intersection of the two filter-large equality sets.

## 3. Restricting to the gluing boundary detects a scalar coequalizer

Let X=[0,kappa], let d be successor with kappa fixed, and put

    Y_0=C(X,omega),       Y_1=omega^X,
    p_Y=inclusion,       q_Y=d^*.

Let theta:E(Y)->U be any presheaf map, with U the reduced power of the template in Section 2. Restrict theta_0 row functions from B to A. Since p_R=q_R on A, this restricted map sends the two functions

    F composed with p_Y,       F composed with q_Y

to the same germ for every F:Y_1->omega.

Let T subset Y_0 consist of the constant functions. There is a function F:Y_1->{0,1,2} such that

    F(p_Y g)=F(q_Y g) iff g belongs to T.                (1)

Indeed, form the undirected graph on Y_1 whose non-loop edges are (p_Y g,q_Y g). Since p_Y is an inclusion, this is the underlying graph of a partial function; each component has at most one cycle and admits a proper three-coloring. Extend these componentwise colorings to F. The loops are exactly the continuous functions satisfying g composed with d=g. Successor induction and continuity at limits show that such functions are constant. This proves (1).

Finite-operation preservation now gives

    (theta_0 chi_T)|_A=1.                               (2)

No scalar point evaluation, stage lifting, or full faithfulness of E was assumed in deriving (2).

## 4. Continuum Boolean continuity upgrades boundary agreement

The M_1-set Y_0 has no fixed points. Hence the [continuum Boolean-continuity theorem](continuum_boolean_continuity_and_orbit_separator_factorization_2026-11-18.md) applies to theta_0.

The constant functions are characterized by

    T = intersection_(m:omega->{0,1}) m^(-1)(T).         (3)

There are c such transformations. If g is nonconstant, a binary transformation separating two values of g witnesses that it fails one of the conditions in (3).

Put f=theta_0(chi_T). Every binary transformation m sends B=omega^3 into A. Thus (2) implies

    f·m=1                   for every binary m.

Equivariance and continuum meet preservation, applied to (3), yield

    f = inf_(binary m) (f·m)=1.                         (4)

In particular theta_0 is supported on the constant source functions, even though p_U itself is not monic.

Let j_0 in E(Y)_0 be evaluation at ordinal 0. For any G:Y_0->omega define u_G(n)=G(constant_n). On T,

    G=u_G composed with j_0.

Equation (4), finite conditionals, and unary equivariance therefore give

    theta_0(G)=u_G(theta_0(j_0)).                        (5)

Thus the entire first-sort map is determined by the single value theta_0(j_0).

## 5. The ordinal seed is epic for this actual target family

Suppose a,b:E(Y)->U agree on j_0. Formula (5) gives a_0=b_0. Naturality then gives

    p_U a_1=p_U b_1,       q_U a_1=q_U b_1.

The joint monicity from Section 2 implies a_1=b_1. Hence a=b.

Therefore this explicit family of actual M_bfs-targets, despite having nonmonic p and c^+-sized finite-support join failures, cannot realize two different ordinal-source maps agreeing on j_0. The obstruction uses both the gluing boundary and continuum Boolean continuity. It is stronger than excluding an ordinary stage-induced moving-cut construction, but it is specific to this template family; arbitrary nonmonic-p M-targets are not covered.

## 6. A finite argument excludes every projection-separating gluing boundary

There is a stronger version of the preceding no-go, which avoids continuum meet preservation. Let n be a positive finite integer, put B=omega^n, and let A subset B be any M_1-subact such that the n coordinate projections have pairwise distinct restrictions to A; when n=1 also assume A is nonempty. Again take

    R_0=B,       R_1=B amalgamated_over_A B,

with the two legs as p_R,q_R. The same character calculation gives R in C_bfs. Let U be any countably complete-filter reduced power of E(R). The countable completeness here suffices for the no-go; the stronger completeness in Section 1 guarantees U in M.

For an ordinal-source map theta, the graph-coloring argument of Section 3 still gives f=theta_0 chi_T equal to 1 on A. Choose finitely many tuples a^1,...,a^k in A whose column vectors

    (a_i^1,...,a_i^k),       0<=i<n,

are pairwise distinct. Such a finite list exists by projection separation. Let r_*=(0,...,n-1), and choose transformations m_j of omega with m_j(r_*)=a^j. They can be extended off {0,...,n-1} so that the combined map

    omega -> omega^k,       x |-> (m_1(x),...,m_k(x))

is injective: choose fresh distinct column vectors for the remaining countably many x. Hence, on the ordinal source,

    T=intersection_(j=1,...,k) m_j^(-1)(T).

Finite-operation preservation yields f=meet_j(f·m_j). Since every a^j belongs to A, this equality makes f(r_*)=1 on a filter-large set of rows.

Moreover T is M_1-stable, so chi_T<=chi_T·m for every m, and therefore

    f<=f·m.                                             (6)

For each r in the countable set B choose m_r with m_r(r_*)=r. Synchronize (6) for this countable family using countable completeness of the filter. On a common large set of rows, f(r_*)=1 forces f(r)=1 for every r in B. Thus f=1 in U.

Formula (5) and joint monicity of p_U,q_U now prove that the ordinal seed j_0 is epic against U exactly as before. For n=1 the projection-separation condition can be read with a nonempty A, which forces A=B; that case is immediate. Empty A cannot give a characterful C-gluing with n>1.

In particular choosing only a few diagonal faces in omega^n does not escape the no-go as long as they separate the coordinate projections, which is precisely the condition needed for this pushout template to belong to C. This excludes a larger family of finite-support gluing targets, but does not address arbitrary countable C-templates.

## 7. A general but weaker restriction for M_bfs targets

There is an additional observation that does not require a gluing template. Let Z be any target with E(sigma)-orthogonality and second-sort finite-support separation; in particular any Z in M_bfs has these properties. Every map theta:E(Y_kappa)->Z factors through restriction of the second source carrier to

    Fin([0,kappa],omega).

Indeed, if F,G on the original second carrier agree on its finite-support points, then F·e_n=G·e_n for every finite-rank truncation e_n. Equivariance and finite-support separation in Z_1 give theta_1(F)=theta_1(G). This defines the required factored second component; the first component is unchanged, and both structural images consist of finite-image functions.

Every ultrafilter V on [0,kappa] defines an equivariant character on Fin([0,kappa],omega), by selecting the unique ultrafilter-large fibre of each finite-image function. For a nonzero limit beta<kappa, take V containing all final segments below beta. Its restrictions along both p and q are evaluation at beta. The principal ultrafilter at beta instead has p-restriction evaluation at beta and q-restriction evaluation at beta+1.

After applying theta, E(sigma)-orthogonality identifies these two second-sort balanced elements because their p-images agree. Taking q-images gives

    theta_0(ev_beta)=theta_0(ev_(beta+1))

for every nonzero limit beta<kappa. This is ZFC; only ordinary ultrafilter extension is used, not countably complete ultrafilters.

These pointwise identities do not establish a simultaneous full-power factorization for arbitrarily large kappa. The number of limit ordinals can exceed the available global c-sized Boolean-continuity bound. Thus this observation does not extend the complete no-go of Sections 3–6 to every M_bfs target.
