# Full Isbell objects in the local factorization classes

2026-11-18. Status: direct ZFC proof, using the already proved finite
orthogonality transfer argument. Neither N nor (NC) is needed. The initial
proof used N; the right-action separation argument below removes it.

Use the actual \(E:\mathcal K^{op}\to\mathcal P\),
\(\mathcal D=\operatorname{Repl}(E(\mathcal C^{op}))\),
\(\mathcal M\), and \(\mathcal H_{\mathrm{fg}}\) from
[the candidate note](mono_factorization_candidate_2026-11-18.md).
No full faithfulness assumption on \(E\) is used below.

## The intrinsic finite-support separation condition

For \(n\geq1\), let \(e_n:\Omega\to\Omega\) fix
\(0,\ldots,n-1\) and send every other integer to \(0\). Consider

\[
z,z'\in Z_0,\quad
(\forall n\geq1,\ z\mathbin{\cdot}e_n=z'\mathbin{\cdot}e_n)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad z=z'.
\tag{FS-sep}
\]

Every \(E(C)\), \(C\in\mathcal C\), satisfies this condition: every
point of \(C_0\) is fixed by some \(e_n\), so two functions agreeing
after precomposition with all \(e_n\) agree everywhere.

The condition transfers to \(\mathcal H_{\mathrm{fg}}\). Factor the
inclusion of the subpresheaf generated by \(z,z'\) through \(E(C)\to Z\).
All the displayed relations already hold in that generated subpresheaf,
so they hold on the chosen lifts in \(E(C)_0\). The lifts are equal by
(FS-sep), hence \(z=z'\).

The condition is also preserved by all small limits and directed unions
of monomorphisms. For limits, test equality after every projection. For
a directed union, put the two elements in one stage; its monic inclusion
reflects every displayed equality back to that stage.

For full Isbell objects, (FS-sep) is equivalent to \(Y_0\) being
finite-support. To prove the nontrivial direction, suppose \(y\in Y_0\)
has no finite support. Then \(e_n y\ne y\) for every \(n\): otherwise
\(e_n y=y\) would give support \(\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\), since
\(m e_n=m'e_n\) whenever \(m,m'\) agree on that set. As \(e_n\) is
idempotent, \(y\) does not belong to its image on \(Y_0\). Consequently
the nonzero function \(\chi_y:Y_0\to\{0,1\}\) satisfies
\(\chi_y\mathbin{\cdot}e_n=0\) for every \(n\), violating (FS-sep).

## Proposition

In ZFC, for every \(Y\in\mathcal K\),

\[
E(Y)\in\mathcal H_{\mathrm{fg}}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
E(Y)\in\mathcal M
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
Y\in\mathcal C.
\]

Thus

\[
\mathcal H_{\mathrm{fg}}\cap
\operatorname{Repl}(E(\mathcal K^{op}))
=\mathcal M\cap\operatorname{Repl}(E(\mathcal K^{op}))
=\mathcal D.
\]

This holds without any size or ambient finite-generation hypothesis on
\(E(Y)\).

## Proof

Only the implication \(E(Y)\in\mathcal H_{\mathrm{fg}}\Rightarrow
Y\in\mathcal C\) needs proof.

First, the [finite-orthogonality lemma](general_equalizers_finite_orthogonality_and_quotient_reduction_2026-11-17.md),
Sections 2–3, gives

\[
\mathcal H_{\mathrm{fg}}\subseteq (E(\sigma))^{\perp},
\]

where \(\sigma:U\to B\) is the fixed test for the condition
\(H_1(p_Y):H_1(Y_1)\to H_1(Y_0)\) being bijective. Consequently
\(H_1(p_Y)\) is bijective. This part is ZFC: the two testing objects
\(B,U\) have countable component carriers, so the required Hom
identifications do not need N.

By (FS-sep) and its full-power characterization, \(Y_0\) is
finite-support. Together with the already proved
\(H_1(p_Y)\) condition this gives \(Y\in\mathcal C\). The converse
implications follow from \(\mathcal D\subseteq\mathcal M\subseteq
\mathcal H_{\mathrm{fg}}\). \(\square\)

## Consequence for retract searches

A retract of an \(\mathcal M\)-object that happens to be a full
Isbell object \(E(Y)\) must already lie in \(\mathcal D\): retract
closure of \(\mathcal H_{\mathrm{fg}}\) puts it in that larger class,
and the proposition applies. Such a retract therefore cannot witness
failure of \(\mathcal M\)-membership.

The same observation applies to any repaired class lying between
\(\mathcal D\) and \(\mathcal H_{\mathrm{fg}}\). This does not prove
general retract closure; any bad retract must remain outside the full
Isbell image.

## The minimal limit-and-union closure

Let \(\mathcal A\) be the least full replete class containing
\(\mathcal D\) and closed under products, equalizers, and directed
unions. This definition does not assume
\(\mathcal A\subseteq\mathcal H_{\mathrm{fg}}\).

Both (FS-sep) and orthogonality to \(E(\sigma)\) are preserved by these
operations. For the latter, limit closure is automatic for an
orthogonality class, and directed-union closure follows because both
endpoints of \(E(\sigma)\) are ambient finitely generated. Hence the
same full-power argument gives, in ZFC,

\[
\mathcal A\cap\operatorname{Repl}(E(\mathcal K^{op}))=\mathcal D.
\]

In particular \(X=E(B)\notin\mathcal A\). This is a genuine invariant
of the repaired construction, but it does not prove nonreflection: a
reflection of \(X\), if it exists, could be an object outside the full
Isbell image.

Indeed, (FS-sep) itself is a small orthogonality condition. Take the free
presheaf on two sort-0 generators, impose the relations
\(z\mathbin{\cdot}e_n=z'\mathbin{\cdot}e_n\) for all \(n\), and then
map this quotient onto the further quotient imposing \(z=z'\).
Orthogonality to this one epimorphism expresses exactly (FS-sep). Together
with \(E(\sigma)\), it defines a small orthogonality class, which is
reflective in the presheaf category. Thus these two intrinsic conditions
alone cannot supply the missing nonreflection proof for \(\mathcal A\).
