# Bounded monic local witnesses force solution sets

2026-11-18. Status: direct ZFC proof. This is a no-go theorem for a restricted
family of Problem 4 constructions, not a proof of the general statement.

## 1. General bounded-witness lemma

Let \(\mathcal P=[\mathcal S^{op},\mathrm{Set}]\), where
\(\mathcal S\) is a small category. Measure the size of a presheaf by the
cardinality of the disjoint union of all its component sets. Let
\(\mathcal M\) be a full replete subcategory with the following property:

> There is an infinite cardinal \(\kappa\geq|\operatorname{Mor}\mathcal S|\)
> and a class \(\mathcal D_0\subseteq\mathcal M\) of presheaves of size
> at most \(\kappa\), such that a presheaf \(Z\) belongs to
> \(\mathcal M\) exactly when every finite family of elements of \(Z\)
> is contained in a \(\mathcal D_0\)-subobject of \(Z\).

Finite families may use different sorts, and include the empty family.

Then every subpresheaf \(S\subseteq Z\), with \(Z\in\mathcal M\), is
contained in an \(\mathcal M\)-subobject \(Z'\subseteq Z\) satisfying

\[
|Z'|\leq\max(|S|,\kappa).
\tag{1}
\]

**Proof.** Write \(\theta=\max(|S|,\kappa)\), and start with
\(S_0=S\). Given a subpresheaf \(S_n\subseteq Z\) of size at most
\(\theta\), choose for each finite family of elements of \(S_n\) one
\(\mathcal D_0\)-subobject of \(Z\) containing that family. There are
at most \(\theta\) finite families, and each chosen subobject has size
at most \(\kappa\leq\theta\). Let \(S_{n+1}\) be the subpresheaf
generated by \(S_n\) and all these images. Its size is still at most
\(\theta\), since the signature has size at most \(\kappa\).

Set \(Z'=\bigcup_{n<\omega}S_n\). Every finite family in \(Z'\) lies
in some \(S_n\), and its chosen \(\mathcal D_0\)-subobject is contained
in \(S_{n+1}\subseteq Z'\). Hence \(Z'\in\mathcal M\), and (1)
follows. The choice for the empty family handles \(S=\varnothing\) as
well. Since all witnesses are subobjects of the fixed presheaf \(Z\),
they range over a set; no proper-class choice is required. \(\square\)

## 2. Solution sets and reflection

For every \(A\in\mathcal P\), the comma category
\(A\downarrow\mathcal M\) has a weakly initial set.

Indeed, any map \(f:A\to Z\), \(Z\in\mathcal M\), factors through its
image subpresheaf, which has size at most \(|A|\). Applying Section 1
gives a factorization

\[
A\longrightarrow Z'\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow Z,
\qquad Z'\in\mathcal M,\quad
|Z'|\leq\max(|A|,\kappa).
\]

There is only a set of isomorphism classes of presheaves of this bounded
size, and a set of maps from \(A\) into their representatives. The maps
whose codomains lie in \(\mathcal M\) form the required weakly initial
set.

If \(\mathcal M\) is closed under small products, it is weakly reflective:
take the product of the targets in a weakly initial set and the diagonal
map from \(A\). If it is also closed under equalizers, it is reflective.
The latter assertion is the usual initial-object lemma applied to the
complete comma category \(A\downarrow\mathcal M\), which has a weakly
initial set.

Consequently, **a monic finite-local construction with a uniform bound on
its local witnesses cannot be a Problem 4 counterexample**: if it has the
required limit closure, it is reflective. Directed-union closure already
follows from its finite-local definition, but is not needed for the
solution-set proof above.

## 3. Application to both-finite-support Isbell witnesses

Let \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{bfs}}\) consist of the objects of the actual
\(\mathcal C\) whose two sorts are both finite-support. Put

\[
\mathcal D_{\mathrm{bfs}}
=\operatorname{Repl}(E(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{bfs}}^{op})),
\]

and let \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{bfs}}\) be the monic finite-local class
defined using \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{bfs}}\).

The [bounded epimorphic compression theorem](both_finite_support_accessibility_and_epi_compression_2026-11-18.md)
shows that every finite family in an \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{bfs}}\)-object
is contained in a subobject \(E(Y')\), where

\[
Y'\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{bfs}},\qquad |Y'_0|+|Y'_1|\leq\mathfrak c.
\]

Such a presheaf has size at most
\(\aleph_0^{\mathfrak c}=2^{\mathfrak c}\). The ambient signature has
size \(\mathfrak c\). Therefore Sections 1–2 apply with
\(\kappa=2^{\mathfrak c}\): every map \(A\to Z\),
\(Z\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{bfs}}\), factors through an
\(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{bfs}}\)-subobject of size at most

\[
\max(|A|,2^{\mathfrak c}).
\]

The class \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{bfs}}\) is product-closed, since
\(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{bfs}}\) is coproduct-closed. Thus
\(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{bfs}}\) is product-closed and weakly reflective.
The subsequent [equalizer-closure theorem](both_finite_support_monic_local_class_is_reflective_2026-11-18.md)
establishes the missing condition in ZFC, even for equalizers whose target
belongs to the unrestricted \(\mathcal M\). Consequently
\(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{bfs}}\) is reflective and does not supply a Problem 4
counterexample.

This conclusion is ZFC and does not use N or (NC). It concerns
\(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{bfs}}\), not the actual unrestricted
\(\mathcal M\). It also does not automatically apply to every subclass
of \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{bfs}}\): the bounded witness produced by
compression must still belong to the chosen defining class.

## 4. What this does not prove

The bounded-witness hypothesis does not by itself imply equalizer closure,
retract closure, or sufficiently-filtered-colimit closure. It gives bounded
subobject hulls and solution sets. Those are enough to force reflection
*if* the requisite limit closure is also present.

The unrestricted Isbell candidate cannot be replaced by the
both-finite-support candidate while retaining the original failure of
weak initial sets. A counterexample using the present nonreflection
mechanism must use witnesses outside this bounded-compression subclass.
