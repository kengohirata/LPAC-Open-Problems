# Finite-support type quotients are completed inside M-equalizers

2026-11-18. Status: PROOF_RECONSTRUCTED; Sections 1–5 independently checked by another research agent, not externally reviewed or formally verified. Section 6 is an additional reconstructed special theorem awaiting audit. This proves a finite-support extension of the Borel epic argument. It gives full-power subobjects inside equalizers, with an explicit remaining C-membership condition. It is not a general proof of M-equalizer closure.

Write M=End(Omega). Let S be a finite-support M-set and let h_1,...,h_k:S->Omega be a finite nonempty family. Define the M-congruence

    s~t iff h_j(m s)=h_j(m t) for every j and m in M,

and let q:S->Q be its quotient. The induced functions on Q are also denoted h_j.

**Theorem (ZFC).** Let Z belong to M, fix one of its sorts, and let a,b:Omega^S->Z_i be maps commuting with both monoid actions. If a(h_j)=b(h_j) for every j, then a and b agree on every function factoring through q:

    a(f q)=b(f q)       for every f:Q->Omega.

The theorem includes finite-support sets with fixed points. No N, NC, or uniform carrier bound on a monic witness is used.

## 1. Countably many coordinates determine the quotient

Choose a countable dense submonoid M_0 of M in the pointwise topology; the eventually constant maps, together with id, suffice. List it as (m_n)_(n in omega), including id. For a finite-support point s, the map m->m s is continuous to the discrete set S. Therefore, for any pair s,t, equality of all h_j(m_n s),h_j(m_n t) implies equality for every m, by choosing an m_n agreeing with m on the union of finite supports of s,t.

Thus the functions

    phi_(j,n)(q)=h_j(m_n q)

separate Q. In particular |Q|<=c. Let B be the sigma-algebra on Q generated by these countably many functions. Every translate h_j·m is B-measurable: approximate m by a sequence from M_0; finite support makes the values eventually agree at each point. The same observation shows that every M-action map on Q is B-measurable.

The equality set of a,b contains all B-measurable Omega-valued functions, pulled back to S. Indeed it contains the initial h_j and all their translates; it is closed under finite pointwise operations and left truncation reconstruction because Z has finite tuple coding and left joint separation. The [sigma-Boolean reduction](truncation_closed_subobjects_and_sigma_boolean_reduction_2026-11-18.md), Section 2, gives the assertion.

## 2. Packing the countable coordinates, including fixed points

Choose injections b_n:Omega->Omega with pairwise disjoint images, and left inverses a_n with a_n b_n=id. Acting on Q, each b_n is injective and

    b_n Q = Fix(b_n a_n).

This is B-measurable: its membership is expressed by equality of all countably many separating coordinates at q and at b_n a_n q. The set Q^M of fixed points is also B-measurable, using the countable dense monoid and the same separating coordinates.

For n!=r,

    b_n Q intersect b_r Q = Q^M.                       (1)

To check the nontrivial inclusion, write z=b_n x=b_r y. For every l in Omega choose t with t b_n=id and t b_r=const_l. Then x=const_l y for every l, so x is fixed by all M. Hence z=b_n x=x is fixed. The reverse inclusion is immediate.

For each j define z_j:Q->Omega by

    z_j(q)=h_j(q)                         on Q^M;
           phi_(j,n)(a_n q)              on b_n Q minus Q^M;
           0                             elsewhere.

The pieces outside Q^M are disjoint by (1), and all pieces are B-measurable. Hence z_j is B-measurable. On fixed points, phi_(j,n)=h_j for every n. Consequently, for all q,

    z_j(b_n q)=phi_(j,n)(q).                           (2)

This packs the countable separating coordinates into the right translates of the finite family z_1,...,z_k. Since these functions are B-measurable, they have common images under a,b.

## 3. A monic local atom forces agreement on every subset of Q

Let U be an arbitrary subset of Q. Suppose a(chi_U q) and b(chi_U q) differ. Their symmetric difference v is then a nonzero binary element of Z_i. Take a monic D-cover of the finite family consisting of v and the k common images of z_j q:

    j:E(T)->Z.

Write v' and z'_j for the corresponding functions on T_i. They are binary where appropriate by monicity. Choose t in T_i with v'(t)=1 and set

    p_(j,n)=(z'_j·b_n)(t).

The nonzero binary element w=j(chi_t) satisfies

    w<=v,       w<=[a(phi_(j,n) q)=p_(j,n)]             (3)

for every j,n, by (2) and finite Boolean operations.

Enumerate the coordinate pairs (j,n). Define the B-measurable first-difference counter H_p:Q->Omega by H_p(q)=0 if all coordinates of q equal p, and otherwise 1 plus the least coordinate at which they differ. Its zero fiber is either empty or a singleton, since the coordinates separate Q. The function H_p q has a common image c_p under a,b.

Every left truncation e_N c_p is a finite operation of finitely many coordinate images. Thus (3) gives

    e_N(if w=1 then c_p else 0)=0       for every N.

Left joint separation makes the conditional element zero. Hence

    w <= [c_p=0].                                     (4)

If H_p has empty zero fiber, the right side is zero, contradicting w!=0. Otherwise let q_0 be its unique zero. Its fiber is B-measurable, so c_0=a(chi_(q^-1{q_0}))=b(chi_(q^-1{q_0})) is a common binary element, and (4) says w<=c_0.

On this fiber, chi_U is the constant chi_U(q_0). The images of chi_U under a and b therefore agree after meeting with c_0; consequently v meet c_0=0. Together with w<=v this again contradicts w!=0. Thus a and b agree on chi_U q for every subset U of Q.

Finally the unary fiber indicators of an arbitrary f:Q->Omega form a point-separating family. Agreement on all corresponding binary functions and left joint separation give a(f q)=b(f q). This proves the theorem.

## 4. Application to a finite seed in an equalizer

Let Y belong to C_bfs, let a,b:E(Y)->Z with Z in M, and take finitely many seed elements in Eq(a,b). In each sort take the finite family consisting of the given elements and the structural images of the sort-1 generators, adding constant zero if that family is empty. Define Q_i by their full M-orbit observation types, as above.

The resulting relations are M-congruences and are respected by p_Y,q_Y, so they define an onto K-map

    q:Y->Q.

Both Q_i are finite-support and have cardinality at most c. Applying the theorem in each sort gives

    E(Q) -> Eq(a,b)

as a monomorphism, containing the prescribed seed. This is a full-power witness, not merely a sigma-Boolean subobject.

If Q belongs to C, this is exactly the required monic D-cover. Thus equalizer closure for these sources is reduced to obtaining a finite-seed type quotient with the C unique-extension condition.

## 5. The C condition remains a genuine step

The image/type quotient of a C-object need not lie in C. The standard quotient

    Z_*=(Omega --id,id--> Omega) -> B=(Omega⇉1)

already illustrates this. E(sigma)-orthogonality of the whole equalizer gives an extension of every balanced element of E(Q)_0, but the extension need not lie in the particular E(Q)_1 just constructed.

Injectivity of H(p_Q) is automatic for every componentwise quotient Y->Q of a C-object: pull two Q_1-characters back along the onto q_1, use injectivity of H(p_Y), then use surjectivity of q_1. Thus only surjectivity of H(p_Q) remains.

One may add such extensions to the seed and repeat the theorem. What has not been proved is that this process reaches a C-quotient which remains covered by finitely many packed observation functions, or that the required simultaneous extensions can all be captured by one stage. No conclusion that Q is automatically in C is made.

This separates two issues: for finite-support sources, M targets force completion from countably observable seed data to the entire full power of the type quotient; the remaining problem is compatibility of the two sorts with C's unique-extension condition.

## 6. A separable-spectrum repair for diagonal sources

Assume now Y=(S --id,id--> S), with S finite-support. For a given finite seed, construct Q_0,Q_1 as in Section 4, so Q_1 is a quotient of Q_0 and E(Q_i) lies in the corresponding equalizer sort. Suppose the compact Stone space H(Q_0) is separable.

Then the seed has a monic D-cover inside the equalizer. If H(Q_0) is empty, H(Q_1) is also empty and the existing quotient Q already belongs to C. Otherwise choose a countable dense family (h_n) in H(Q_0). Each h_n, pulled back to S, is a balanced element of the first equalizer sort. E(sigma)-orthogonality of the equalizer extends it to the second sort. Because the source structural map is the identity, this extension is the very same function on S.

Refine Q_1 to Q'_1 by adding these countably many functions to its defining observation family. They all factor through Q_0, so Q'_1 is still a quotient of Q_0. If two points of Q_0 have the same images under all h_n, they have equal values under every h in H(Q_0): the equality condition is closed in H(Q_0) and contains the dense family. Hence every character of Q_0 descends to Q'_1. The induced quotient

    Q'=(Q_0 --p,p--> Q'_1)

therefore belongs to C.

It remains to check that E(Q'_1) lies in the second equalizer sort. Its defining observation family is countable and its sigma-algebra is contained in that equalizer. Moreover Q'_1 has a character, so it has no M-fixed point. Consequently the packing argument of Section 2 packs the entire countable observation family into one sigma-measurable function: the sets b_n Q'_1 are now disjoint, and no fixed-point compatibility condition is needed. Its right translates include all the observations, so its type quotient is exactly Q'_1. Applying the main theorem to this one common packed function proves that all of E(Q'_1) lies in the equalizer. Together with E(Q_0) in sort 0, this gives the asserted monic E(Q') cover.

**Corollary (ZFC).** For every set I, every Z in M, and every pair

    a,b:E(I·Z_*) -> Z,

their equalizer belongs to M.

Indeed every quotient of the M-set I times Omega is a disjoint union of Omega-orbits and fixed points. Its character space is empty if there is a fixed point, and otherwise is a singleton, since Omega has exactly one equivariant map to itself. Thus every finite-seed Q_0 in this situation has separable character space, and the preceding repair applies. The resulting witnesses have both sorts finite-support.

The diagonal hypothesis in this section is essential to the proof as written. For a general C_bfs source, adding a sort-1 extension also adds its pullback along q_Y to the sort-0 observation family. This can enlarge H(Q_0); it has not been shown that separability survives or that a countable collection of dynamical iterates suffices.
