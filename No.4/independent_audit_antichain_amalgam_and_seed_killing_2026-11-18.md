## 1. Controlled intrinsic amalgam

The normalized witness is an actual C-object in ZFC. Its first sort is a singleton, and its second sort has a fixed point obtained by collapsing all finite-image maps in all tail copies. Both relevant Hom spaces into Ω are empty, so the required H(p) is bijective. The quotient from the tail copies of Y_ω respects both arrows because their images consist of finite-image maps. Its dual has first sort Ω. Right multiplication by every finite-image transformation sends every second-sort function to its value at the collapsed point, viewed as a constant.

The countably complete Boolean algebra description was checked. The bounded-row ideal is countably complete, and countable partitions in its quotient lift to partitions on one tail. Thus the partition functor Γ gives exactly the component sets of W, with their actions and finite operations.

The algebra A generated by the seed's binary fibres, countable Boolean operations, and all right actions has size at most c: closure for ω₁ stages suffices and c^ω=c. Its chosen representatives are constant on all finite-image transformations, uniformly in the row. There are at most c operation and action equations, including all countable joins. Since λ=c⁺ is regular, these equations hold simultaneously on one tail. This gives the stated homomorphism into P(Y₁), and injectivity follows by composing with the inclusion into the original quotient algebra. This is a genuine simultaneous lift of this small algebra, not a lifting assertion about an arbitrary quotient homomorphism.

The Boolean pushout step uses the stated strong-amalgamation theorem for countably complete Boolean algebras. The cited theorem and its scope were checked in the primary Chen source during the original audit; its general proof is not reproduced here. Both pushout legs are therefore embeddings. The right actions, two structure maps, and the retraction extend by the pushout universal property. Applying Γ preserves both embeddings. The first-sort embedding Ω→W₀ consists of constants and is injective.

The identity u·e_n=(sru)·e_n is valid: the corresponding two Boolean homomorphisms agree on the W summand, and on the new power summand the finite-image identity makes both values equal to the same constant. For a balanced u, this becomes e_n·u=e_n·sru for every n. The truncations are jointly point-separating, and Γ has the corresponding joint-separation property, so u=sru. This proves that there are no new balanced elements in sort 1. Sort 0 was unchanged. The standard finite-tuple description of maps from E(B) and E(U) then preserves E(σ)-orthogonality. The claimed intrinsic axioms and split extension are valid.

## 2. Packing row functions into one generator

For at most c functions f_i vanishing on finite-image transformations, injections b_i with pairwise almost-disjoint infinite ranges give z·b_i=f_i. The definition of z cannot conflict on infinite-image transformations: a common infinite image would lie in a finite intersection of two ranges. It cannot conflict on finite-image transformations either, since all prescribed values there are zero. Injectivity of each b_i handles uniqueness within one prescription. Thus the packing is pointwise, not merely modulo the tail ideal.

For a binary pattern p with p(id)=1, at most one row can realize the entire pattern at a point in A_t. Such a point is a permutation; two realizing rows would be right translates by the quotient of their realizing permutations. Deleting that one possible row leaves a tail on which every point in A_t has a chosen mismatch μ(t,r).

The counter H_m is zero when μ=m, and otherwise one plus the first differing coordinate. Its displayed truncation relation is correct: e_N fixes values below N, and the corresponding tuple operation searches precisely coordinates j<N−1. Outside A_t, both sides are zero. The second tuple relation excludes simultaneous X=1, H_m=0, and X_m=p(m), exactly by the chosen mismatch.

There are at most c functions, including every required tuple code, so all are right translates of the single z. Choosing the tuple codes to send the zero tuple to zero ensures that they retain the finite-image vanishing needed for packing. Consequently all relations used later are actual unary equations in the subpresheaf generated by z.

## 3. A single generator for all singleton comparisons

Suppose a:W→Z, with Z∈H_fg, and a(x)=j(h)≠0 for a monomorphism j:E(Y)→Z. Applying the unary retraction onto {0,1} and using monicity makes h binary. Maps preserve the canonical constants, so h≠0. Choose y with h(y)=1, set v=χ_{y}, and set p(m)=h(my). Then j(v) is nonzero.

The encoding d(z)=c(v(αz),h(βz)) was checked with the right-action convention. For t_m(n)=c(n,m(n)), the identities αt_m=id and βt_m=m give d·t_m=c(v,h·m). Its two projections and the unary equality-test relation recover v, h·m, and v≤[h·m=p(m)]. Thus all c singleton comparisons are unary equations generated by one element d; no countable or c-sized generating family is being smuggled into the finitely generated test.

## 4. The two-generator test and contradiction

Let G⊂Z be generated by j(d) and a(z). It contains every needed tuple-code element, j(v), and every a(X_m). The two definitions of the latter agree because j(h)=a(x). Since Z∈H_fg, the inclusion factors through a full-power object E(Y′). The first map of this factorization is injective, since its composite is the inclusion. Thus the image v′ is binary and nonzero.

Evaluate at a point y′ with v′(y′)=1. The equations involving d force X′_m(y′)=p(m) for every m, including X′(y′)=1. Define m_*(n)=C′_n(y′). The finite tuple equations for each N and the truncation relation imply e_N(H′_{m_*}(y′))=0 for every N≥2. Joint point separation of these truncations gives H′_{m_*}(y′)=0. The remaining tuple relation then evaluates to 1=0.

The factorization preserves the needed unary equations automatically. It does not have to preserve arbitrary externally specified operations, and G does not have to be finitely presentable. No cardinal bound on Y or Y′, no stage lifting, no N, and no NC enters this contradiction.

## 5. Checked conclusions and precise limits

The audited theorem is the following ZFC statement: if Z∈H_fg and a:W→Z, then a(x) cannot be a nonzero element of a monic E(Y)-image, for any K-object Y. Hence every W→M kills x. In particular W cannot embed into, or be a retract of, an M-object.

The controlled amalgam has embeddings of W and of its normalized D-witness agreeing on the nonzero seed. The theorem therefore proves that this amalgam is outside H_fg, despite satisfying the listed intrinsic axioms. There is no conflict between the two constructions.

Neither statement proves equalizer or retract closure of M. They decisively exclude this particular W from every proposed embedding or retract-envelope route to a counterexample.
