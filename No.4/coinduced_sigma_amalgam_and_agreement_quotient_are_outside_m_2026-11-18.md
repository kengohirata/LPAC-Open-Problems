# Coinduced sigma-amalgams and agreement quotients are outside M

2026-11-18. Status: PROOF_RECONSTRUCTED. This gives a two-generator obstruction to M-membership for two concrete targets. It does not assert nonmembership in H_fg. Everything is ZFC; N is unnecessary.

## 1. The source and the two proposed targets

Let M=End(ω), S=ω^M, with (m·s)(r)=s(rm), and A=S_fs. As in [the observation-profile note, Section 6](omega_closed_observation_profiles_countable_isolation_barrier_2026-11-18.md),

    Y=(A --inc,inc--> S)∈C,
    |A|=c,       |S|=2^c.

Write h_m(s)=s(m), and let Σ⊂P(S) be the sigma-algebra generated by their fibers. It is stable under inverse images of the M-action.

The first proposed Boolean target is the pushout in sigma-complete Boolean algebras

    B_am=P(S) ⊔_Σ P(S),

with maps α,β. Their common coordinate functions give a common h=h_id after passage to countable partitions.

The second target is explicit. On S×S put

    R_J={(s,t):s|J=t|J}       for countable J⊂M,
    I={U⊂S×S:U∩R_J=∅ for some countable J⊂M},
    B_q=P(S×S)/I.

I is a proper sigma-ideal. A countable union has the union of the countable witnesses as witness, and every diagonal pair belongs to every R_J. Inverse image under m sends a mismatch on J to a mismatch on Jm={rm:r∈J}; thus I is M-invariant.

Coordinate pullback supplies maps α,β:P(S)→B_q agreeing on Σ. Consequently there is a canonical homomorphism B_am→B_q respecting α,β.

For either B, set Z_1=Part_ω(B), the countable partitions of 1 with the usual left M_2-action, and Z_0=ω^A. The two structure maps p_Z=q_Z are induced by restriction to A: for B_am use the common restriction of both copies of P(S), and for B_q restrict subsets to the diagonal {(a,a):a∈A}. The latter kills I. The actions are compatible, so this defines an actual P-object Z.

There are two maps a,b:E(Y)⇉Z, both the identity in sort 0 and induced by α,β in sort 1. They agree on h and its full right orbit. Neither target will belong to M.

## 2. A nonzero rectangle with zero point pieces

Let 0∈S be the constant-zero profile. For each m∈M let ε_m∈S be the function equal to 1 at m and 0 elsewhere. Put

    E={ε_m:m∈M},
    d=α({0})∧β(E)∈B.

In B_q, d is represented by the rectangle {0}×E. For every countable J⊂M choose m∉J. Then (0,ε_m)∈R_J. Hence d≠0 in B_q, and therefore also in B_am.

On the other hand, for each m,

    d∧β({ε_m})=0.                                      (1)

Indeed α({0}) lies below the common coordinate event [h_m=0], whereas β({ε_m}) lies below the common event [h_m=1]. This proves (1) in the amalgam itself, without using the quotient model.

Thus d is positive although its point pieces indexed by E are all zero after intersection with d. The crucial fact is that E has cardinality c and can be separated by a countable family.

## 3. Packing the countable separation into one element

Choose an injection M→2^ω. Let g_n:S→{0,1} encode its n-th bit on ε_m, and set g_n=0 outside E. These functions separate E.

Every M-fixed point of S is a constant profile, and no ε_m is constant. Therefore every g_n is 0 on the fixed-point set.

Choose injections b_n:ω→ω with pairwise disjoint ranges and left inverses. For any M-set, two distinct images b_nS,b_rS can intersect only in fixed points: if u=b_nx=b_ry, transformations that are the identity on im(b_n) and constant k on im(b_r) give u=c_ky for all k, hence u is fixed. The corresponding x,y then equal u.

Consequently there is one z:S→ω satisfying

    z·b_n=g_n       for all n.

Define z(b_ns)=g_n(s); this is consistent on overlaps because all the g_n are zero on fixed points, and define z arbitrarily elsewhere.

The finite obstruction will consist of the two sort-1 elements d and β(z).

## 4. A monic D-cover of those two elements is impossible

Suppose Z∈M. Choose a monic D-cover

    j:E(T)↪Z,       T∈C,

containing d and β(z). Let d',z' be their lifts on T_1. Since d is a nonzero binary element and j is monic, choose t∈T_1 with d'(t)=1. Put

    w=j(χ_t)≠0,
    p_n=(z'·b_n)(t).

Then w≤d and

    w≤[β(g_n)=p_n]       for every n.                   (2)

For this profile p define H_p:S→ω by setting it to zero outside E, to zero at a point of E whose full bit profile is p, and otherwise to 1 plus the first differing bit index. The set

    E∩H_p^−1{0}

is empty or a singleton {ε_m}, because the g_n separate E.

Each left truncation e_N H_p is a finite operation of χ_E and finitely many g_n. By w≤d≤β(E) and (2), every truncation of the conditional element

    if w=1 then β(H_p) else 0

vanishes. Left joint separation in Z therefore makes this conditional element zero. Hence

    w≤β(E∩H_p^−1{0}).

If that set is empty, w=0. If it is {ε_m}, combine the inequality with w≤d and (1), again obtaining w=0. Both contradict the choice of w.

Thus no monic D-cover of {d,β(z)} exists, and Z∉M.

The first-difference counter may be chosen after the cover point t and need not lie in the chosen D-subobject. Its image under β is available in Z; only finite operations and left joint separation are used for the conditional argument.

## 5. Consequence and scope

The concrete countable-agreement quotient and the sigma-Boolean pushout both furnish distinct maps E(Y)⇉Z agreeing on the one observation h. Their target fails the required M-membership by the explicit finite seed above.

The full observation space S has omega-closed coordinate image and no countable coordinate family separates all of S. Nevertheless the rectangle {0}×E exposes a smaller positive part whose c many point pieces are separated by countably many observations and individually annihilated. This is why the global omega-closedness does not protect these target constructions.

No closure under uncountable meets or joins was assumed in the argument. No claim that arbitrary intrinsic targets, or these targets' H_fg membership, have been decided is made.
