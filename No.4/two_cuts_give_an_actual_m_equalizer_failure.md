# Two ordinal cuts give an actual failure of equalizer closure for M

Status: a ZFC proof reconstructed in this investigation, checked directly by its authoring agent. No separate independent or formal audit has been performed. The specified small endpoint and the larger endpoint have different conclusions below.

Write c=2^aleph₀. Let Y_κ=(S₀⇉S₁) have S₀=Cont([0,κ],ω), S₁=ω^[0,κ], p inclusion, and qg=g∘d where d is successor with κ fixed. Write u_ξ for first-sort evaluation. All actions on the carriers are postcomposition by End(ω). A map out of E(Y_κ) must respect both monoid actions and both structural arrows.

**Theorem.** At κ=(2^c)^+ there is a countable T∈C_bfs, a proper c^+-complete filter on pairs of ordinals, a target U=(E(T))^I/filter∈M_bfs, and a map θ:E(Y_κ)→U for which θ₀(u_ω) and θ₀(u_κ) are distinct. A second map agrees on u₀ and differs at u_κ. Their equalizer is outside M, although the domain and codomain belong to M. Thus M is not closed under equalizers in ZFC.

At the originally specified λ=c^+, every countable list of observations admits the separating countable local model in Section 2. The assembly in Sections 3–4 gives a presheaf map splitting the endpoint there as well, but its filter has lower completeness. Section 6 shows why the resulting image cannot be used to obtain a split in M by postcomposition. The existence of a different map into M_bfs at exactly c^+ is open in this note.

## 1. One fixed countable template

Let T₀=ω². Start with ω⁵ in the second sort, with the coordinates viewed in the order

    before t, at t, between t and s, at s, after s.

Quotient ω⁵ by the equivalence relation generated, for all x,y∈ω, by the two pairs

    (x,x,y,y,y) ~ (x,x,x,x,y),       (x,y,y,y,y) ~ (x,x,x,y,y).

This equivalence is already invariant under postcomposition, so it is the required monoid congruence. Let T₁ be the quotient. Define p_T(x,y) by the common class of the first pair and q_T(x,y) by that of the second.

Every equivariant ω⁵→ω is a coordinate projection. Indeed at (0,1,2,3,4) its value must be one of those coordinates and this tuple generates the whole act. For a projection to descend through the first pair it must avoid coordinates 3 and 4 (numbering from 1). For the second pair it must avoid 2 and 3. Exactly projections 1 and 5 survive. On p_T both restrict to the two different coordinate projections of ω², which are all of its characters. Hence H(p_T) is bijective. Both carriers are countable and finite-support by construction. Thus T∈C_bfs. The two surviving characters have identical restrictions along p_T and q_T.

## 2. A countable local model for every countable observation list

Let κ be either cardinal in the statement. Take L to be the set of nonzero limit ordinals t with ω<t<κ. For t∈L define the step

    g_t(x,y)(ξ)=x for ξ≤t and y for ξ>t.

It is continuous. Its shift Dg_t=g_t∘d has value x strictly below t and y from t on. For t<s in L, define H_(t,s):ω⁵→S₁ by assigning the five entries to the five pieces displayed above. This is equivariant. Its values on the two sides of the first relation are g_t(x,y),g_s(x,y), and on the second relation Dg_t(x,y),Dg_s(x,y).

For a second-sort observation F:S₁→ω, color each t by the ordered pair of tables

    (F(g_t(x,y)), F(Dg_t(x,y)))_(x,y∈ω).

There are at most c colors for a single F. If t,s have the same color then F H_(t,s) descends to an ω-valued function on T₁. The same-color condition includes every x,y, so it automatically works for all right translates of F. For first-sort observations G:S₀→ω, the table (G(g_t(x,y)))_(x,y∈ω) also has at most c colors.

Any countable family of these colors has at most c possible combined values. Since |L|=κ>c there are distinct t<s of the same combined color, as far out as prescribed. For a precise local-model statement, form the quotient Q of Y_κ by the value profiles of the chosen observations and their right translates, adjoining their structural pullbacks in sort 0 and the observations u_ω,u_κ. The map (x,y)↦[g_t(x,y)] into Q₀ and the map induced by H_(t,s) into Q₁ define T→Q. The latter descends because all defining observations and their translates equate both generating pairs. Both structural squares follow from the formulas for g_t,Dg_t. Its pullbacks of u_ω and u_κ are x and y. Equivalently there is a genuine map on the entire subobject E(Q) of E(Y_κ) giving this distinction in E(T).

This works even for observation lists selected adversarially from the full two sorts. It does not assert compatibility of the choices of t,s as the list grows.

## 3. A filter making the individual choices compatible

Take I={(t,s)∈L²:t<s}. For κ=(2^c)^+ put μ=2^c. For κ=c^+ put μ=c. On I take the filter generated by

    { (t,s): t>β and f(t)=f(s) },       β<κ, f:L→μ.

For the large endpoint this filter is closed under intersections of at most c members: combine at most c colors into one using μ^c=μ and take the supremum of the bounds. For the small endpoint it is closed under countable intersections by c^ω=c. In either case the basic sets are nonempty since every tail of L has κ>μ points. This also proves properness after taking the stated intersections.

Form U as the two-sort reduced power of E(T) by this filter. Equality in each sort is equality of full row functions on a filter-large set. For G:S₀→ω define θ₀(G) at (t,s) by the table G(g_t(x,y)) on T₀. For F:S₁→ω, use the descent of F H_(t,s) to T₁ whenever t and s have the same F-color from Section 2. Else assign the constant zero row. The good rows for each F form a filter-large set, so the arbitrary value on bad rows has no effect on its germ.

These formulas define a P-map. In sort 0 the monoid equations hold rowwise. In sort 1, to check one left or right action equation, intersect the good-row sets of the function and its transform. On that intersection the desired equation follows from the ordinary pointwise postcomposition or from equivariance of H_(t,s), and the quotient ω⁵↠T₁ is onto. For the two structural equations evaluate a good F-row on p_T(x,y) and q_T(x,y). The values are F(g_t(x,y)) and F(Dg_t(x,y)), precisely the two required first-sort rows. No simultaneous good row for every function was used.

For every fixed β<κ, θ₀(u_β) is the germ of the constant row π₁(x,y)=x. The filter contains t>β. In contrast θ₀(u_κ) is the germ of π₂(x,y)=y. These two germs differ because no row has the two coordinate functions equal. There is also a standard P-map b into U obtained on every row from the K-map T→Y_κ assigning the constant ordinal function with value π₁ on T₀ and with value the first surviving character on T₁. This gives b₀(u_β)=π₁ for every β≤κ. In particular θ and b agree on the whole initial seed E(B)→E(Y_κ): in its first sort the seed is generated under left postcomposition by u₀, and its second sort consists of constant functions.

## 4. At the larger endpoint the target belongs to M_bfs

For the proper c^+-complete filter in the large construction, take any finite list of elements of U, choosing full row representatives. Since T₀,T₁ are countable, there are at most c different tables for that finite list. Discard the union of all table types whose occurrence set is filter-small. The discarded set is small by c^+-completeness. Every remaining type has a positive occurrence set, meaning its complement is not filter-large.

Take the coproduct of one copy of T for every remaining type. Its dual maps to U by assigning row copies of T to their corresponding summands. Arbitrary assignments on discarded rows are harmless. The map is monic: any difference on one summand survives throughout its positive occurrence set. Its image contains the original finite list. The coproduct belongs to C_bfs since both sorts remain finite-support and restriction on character spaces is the product of bijections. This proves U∈M_bfs⊂M, also for the empty list. It is the [complete-filter target argument](complete_filter_targets_and_repeated_coordinate_gluing_no_go_2026-11-18.md) applied with its required completeness.

## 5. Why this also rejects M in ZFC

We spell out the size issue in applying the [minimal-hull argument](ordinal_evaluation_has_no_proper_d_hull_2026-11-18.md). All carriers in this application lie below a fixed finite iterate of the powerset cardinal operation starting at ℵ₀, including the carriers of any Q with a monomorphism E(Q)↪E(Y_κ). For the latter assertion, each Q_i injects by distinct singleton indicators into ω^(Q_i), which injects into ω^(Y_i); the latter has the stated bound since κ=(2^c)^+≤2^(2^c) and Y_i⊂ω^[0,κ].

In ZFC every countably complete ultrafilter on a set bounded in size by any such finite iterate is principal. Induct on the iterate. The countable base case follows by countable completeness. If the result holds through μ, pushing a countably complete ultrafilter forward along any function to μ forces a selected fiber. Therefore it is closed under intersections of at most μ members (use the partition by the first failed condition, plus the intersection). On a set of size at most 2^μ, intersect the chosen sides of μ separating bits to obtain a singleton.

Every equivariant ω^A→ω is evaluation at a countably complete ultrafilter: finite tuple coding gives its Boolean ultrafilter on indicators, and the value of each ω-valued function selects one cell of the corresponding countable partition. Thus for all the A just bounded, every such functional is point evaluation. The empty source-index case has no equivariant functional to ω. In particular the second character space of Y_κ consists exactly of the ordinal evaluations. The first is the same compact ordinal by the compact clopen-partition argument, so Y_κ∈C and E(Y_κ)∈D⊂M.

Any monomorphism j:E(Q)↪E(Y_κ), Q∈C, is consequently E(v) for a componentwise-surjective K-map v:Y_κ↠Q: evaluate j at each point of Y_i to obtain a point of Q_i, and use all functions on Q_i to verify equivariance and the two squares. A missed point of Q_i would contradict monicity by its singleton indicator.

If the image contains u₀, that evaluation descends to a character on Q₀ by surjectivity of v₀. The image of H(Q₀) in [0,κ] is closed by finite support and compactness. It contains 0. If it contains ξ, extend the corresponding character over p_Q, pull the extension back along v₁, and use the characterization of H(Y₁) above to identify that pullback with evaluation at ξ. The q-square now puts d(ξ) in the same closed set. A closed successor-stable set containing 0 is [0,κ]. All evaluations thus descend in both sorts and separate both carriers, making v injective as well as onto. This proves the needed minimal-hull property locally in ZFC, with no assumption about measurable cardinals elsewhere.

Now Eq(θ,b) contains u₀ but omits u_κ. If it were in M, its one-element family {u₀} would have a monic E(Q)-cover inside it. The composite into E(Y_κ) would be the whole source by the preceding paragraph, contradicting omission of u_κ. Hence Eq(θ,b)∉M. This is an equalizer counterexample for the actual candidate M. It disqualifies that candidate from answering Problem 4, whose hypotheses require all small limits.

## 6. What happens to the same construction at c^+

At κ=c^+, fix an injection L→2^c. Color each ordered pair t<s by the first coordinate on which their bit strings differ. Each of the c color classes is filter-small: the rows agreeing on that coordinate form a basic large set. Thus the filter is not c^+-complete. More strongly the failure is visible using observations from E(Y_κ) itself.

Call a function of the form H_(t,s)(a,b,c₀,d,e) active at t when (a,b,c₀) is nonconstant, and active at s when (c₀,d,e) is nonconstant. If both are active, the ordered pair t,s is uniquely determined by the function. Indeed the limit points where the function differs from its constant left neighborhood at the point or at its immediate successor are exactly t and s. Each piece between the cuts is infinite. Let A be the set of all such doubly active functions in S₁ and partition A into its c classes A_j by the first differing coordinate of the uniquely determined pair.

For each j, θ₁(χ_(A_j))=0: on good rows with matching j-th bit, a tuple active twice would have precisely that row pair as its cuts, while tuples active at most once never belong to A_j. But θ₁(χ_A)≠0, since a twice-active tuple exists at every pair and the defining relations of T₁ involve only single cuts or constants. If k maps the image of θ (or a subpresheaf containing it) to Z∈M, continuum Boolean continuity of kθ on the fixed-point-free S₁ now forces kθ₁(χ_A)=0.

For clarity, this vanishing forces the endpoints themselves to become equal. Define F on the singly active functions to return the value *at* their unique active limit cut. On constant ordinal functions return their constant value and define F arbitrarily elsewhere. On a singly active or constant function r the identity F(mr)=mF(r) holds for every m: relabeling either preserves the sole visible cut or makes the function constant. Outside the set of functions representable with at most two cuts, all indicators have zero θ₁-image. Therefore, for each m, the Boolean indicator of an equivariance defect has θ₁-image bounded by θ₁(χ_A), and its image under kθ₁ vanishes. Equivalently kθ₁(F) is balanced. In this argument preservation of binary inequalities after applying k can be checked directly on kθ and the input subsets: the complement of the representable set has zero θ-image and A has zero kθ-image. No closure of an intermediate subpresheaf under new operations is required.

On p-images of step functions F takes x; on q-images it takes y. On both legs v₀, the initial second evaluation, takes x. Thus kθ₁(F) and kθ₁(v₀) are balanced with identical p-image, and their q-images are kθ₀(u_κ) and kθ₀(u_ω). Unique balanced extension in Z identifies them and the two endpoint images agree. In particular U at the small endpoint is outside M, and even postprocessing the image of this θ cannot give the sought map there.

The countable-fragment models, the globally coherent map at the larger endpoint, and the failure of this same assembly at c^+ concern different quantifiers. The last argument rules out this construction at c^+ without deciding whether another construction can work there.

## Finite-palette check for a future small-endpoint test

For an End(ω)-set V write V[3] for the union of the images of all endomorphisms of rank at most three acting on V. If V is finite-support, restriction H(V)→H(V[3]) is bijective. Here is the argument. Given a character on V[3] and a point z with a nonempty finite support P, apply the character to z relabeled by each 0–1 indicator on P. This declares a subset of P selected when the answer is 1. The answer depends only on the labels on P. On every partition of P into at most three cells, the corresponding ternary relabeling gives one selected cell (the answer belongs to the finite set of labels). Its further binary relabelings show that exactly the unions containing that cell are selected among unions of those cells. In particular disjoint A,B have their union selected precisely when one is selected, using the partition A,B,P minus their union. This rule and complementation give an ultrafilter on finite P, hence one selected point. Enlarging the support leaves that point unchanged, since the binary observations still depend only on P. Relabeling by m transports the selected point to its m-image, as can be tested by all binary indicators on m(P). The selected values therefore define an equivariant extension to V, unique by the same binary tests. On points of V[3] these tests give back the given character by its equivariance. Empty supports can be enlarged; a fixed point already prevents a character on V[3].

For any target Z in M_bfs quotient each sort by x~y when x·m=y·m for every rank-at-most-three m. This is a presheaf congruence since such m form a two-sided ideal. Each monic local cover E(V)↪Z induces a monic E(V[3]) into this quotient: the relation restricted to its image is precisely agreement on V[3], and functions on V[3] extend set-theoretically to V. Here V[3] is applied in both sorts. The character calculation preserves bijectivity of H(p), so the quotient is again in M_bfs. The quotient is rank-three separated: if all rank-three translates of the classes of x and y agree, for each m of rank at most three choose n of rank at most three with mn=m by selecting one representative for each fiber of m. The quotient criterion on the classes of x·m and y·m, evaluated at n, yields x·m=y·m in Z. This is precisely equality of the original classes.

Distinct balanced endpoint images remain distinct after this quotient: for balanced x,y, binary right translates are binary left relabelings, and the latter jointly separate distinct values, as is checked in a common monic power cover. Any composed source map therefore factors through restriction to Y[3], by rank-three separation (the structural maps take Y₀[3] into Y₁[3]). Conversely any separating map from E(Y[3]) gives a separating map after precomposing with restriction. For the ordinal function carriers, V[3] consists exactly of the functions taking at most three distinct values in ω. Thus the three-value restriction yields an equivalent small-endpoint existence test.

Two-value restriction need not preserve C_bfs. In the explicit T above, in the rank-two part of T₁ one may select x on the two p-tuples and y on the two q-tuples. They have distinct unordered bipartitions of the five positions except for the two imposed pair identifications. Complete this selector independently on every other bipartition by choosing one side, and select the common entry on constant tuples. It is equivariant on the rank-two part and descends through the relations. Its restriction to p is the first coordinate, also the restriction of the first-coordinate character, but its q-restriction is the second coordinate. Thus uniqueness fails there.
