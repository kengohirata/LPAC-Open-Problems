# The Borel amalgam equalizer target is outside M

2026-11-18. Status: PROOF_RECONSTRUCTED; independent audit requested. This rejects the natural sigma-Boolean pushout as an M-codomain for the Borel equalizer construction, in ZFC. It does not decide H_fg membership of this codomain.

Let I=2^omega, let Sigma be its Borel sigma-algebra, and form the pushout of countably complete Boolean algebras

    B=P(I) coproduct_Sigma P(I),

with embeddings alpha,beta:P(I)->B. Strong amalgamation makes both maps injective and their intersection precisely the common copy of Sigma; see the source for this theorem in [the controlled amalgam note](antichain_seed_controlled_intrinsic_amalgam_2026-11-18.md).

Write Gamma(B) for the M_2-set of Omega-valued countable partitions of B. Define a P-object T by

    T_0=T_1=Gamma(B)^Omega,       p_T=q_T=id,

with M_1 acting on the displayed Omega index by precomposition and M_2 acting pointwise. The two maps induced by alpha and beta are

    E(I times Omega --id,id--> I times Omega) -> T.

Their equalizer consists exactly of the Borel functions I times Omega->Omega: equality of the two partition images means equality on every fiber, and the intersection property identifies the fibers in the equalizer as Borel sets.

The object T has finite tuple coding, left joint separation, first-sort FS-separation, E(sigma)-orthogonality, and countable Boolean operations. Nevertheless T does not belong to M.

## 1. A nonzero element invisible at every Borel point

Choose S subset I which is not Borel. Then alpha(S)!=beta(S), so

    v=alpha(S) symmetric-difference beta(S)

is a nonzero Boolean element of B. For every i in I, the common singleton c_i=alpha({i})=beta({i}) satisfies

    v meet c_i=0.                                      (1)

Let C_n={i in I:i(n)=1}, and let b_n=alpha(C_n)=beta(C_n). For every i in I,

    meet_n b_n^(i(n)) = c_i,                           (2)

where b^1=b and b^0=not b. This uses countable completeness and preservation of countable meets by the two pushout legs.

## 2. A single seed exposes the obstruction

Regard v and b_n as binary elements of Gamma(B). Define f in T_1 by

    f(0)=v,       f(n+1)=b_n.

The right constant maps on Omega recover the constant sequences v and each b_n from f. Thus the ambient subpresheaf generated by f contains all these elements.

Suppose j:E(Q)->T is a monomorphism, for any K-object Q, whose image contains f. Let h in Omega^(Q_1) lift f. Its right constant translates lift the constant sequences v and b_n; write these lifts as v' and b'_n. Monicity shows that they are binary. Since v!=0, v'!=0. Choose q in Q_1 with v'(q)=1, and define i in 2^omega by

    i(n)=b'_n(q).

Put a=chi_{q} in Omega^(Q_1). In the source,

    a<=v',       a<=(b'_n)^(i(n)) for every n.

The target T has unique finite tuple coding, so j preserves finite Boolean operations and these inequalities. Therefore, pointwise in the sequence index,

    j(a)<=v meet meet_n b_n^(i(n))
         =v meet c_i
         =0

by (1) and (2). Thus j(a)=0, contradicting monicity because a!=0.

No preservation of countable operations by j was assumed: the individual finite Boolean inequalities suffice, and their countable meet is taken in the target. No finite-support property of Q, no compression theorem, and no N or NC assumption is needed.

Consequently no monic full-power image contains f. In particular T is outside M. The displayed equalizer construction therefore does not yield an actual equalizer counterexample for M.

## 3. Why this does not yet exclude H_fg

In an arbitrary factorization through E(Q), singleton indicators in the source can be sent to zero. The proof above uses monicity precisely to keep j(chi_q) nonzero. Hence the same argument does not prove T outside H_fg.

The outstanding membership question for H_fg would require additional control of these atom-killing maps. It is separate from the now resolved M-nonmembership, which already rejects T as the requested equalizer codomain.

## 4. The Borel inclusion is epic relative to every M-target

There is a stronger ZFC conclusion. Let D_0=E(I times Omega --id,id--> I times Omega), let A be its Borel subobject, and let a,b:D_0->Z with Z in M. If a and b agree on A, then a=b. Thus A cannot be a proper equalizer with any M-codomain, not only with the particular sigma-Boolean amalgam above.

First fix a sort and a subset S of I, viewed as a binary function constant in the Omega index. If the two images differ, their symmetric difference v is a nonzero binary element. The common Borel element f(i,n)=i(n) supplies, by right constant translates, every common binary bit b_n. Take a monic D-cover of the finite family (v,f). As in Section 2, a source point where the lift of v is 1 gives a nonzero binary element z in Z and a point i in 2^omega such that

    z<=v,       z<=b_n^(i(n)) for every n.               (3)

No countable meets in Z are needed. The Borel function H_i:I->Omega, equal to 0 at i and to 1 plus the first differing coordinate elsewhere, has a common image h_i under a and b. Every left truncation e_N h_i is a finite operation of the first finitely many common bits. Thus (3) makes every left truncation of

    d = if z=1 then h_i else 0

equal to zero. Left joint separation gives d=0, so z<=[h_i=0], the common image of the singleton {i}. But v is zero on this common singleton, as in (1). This contradicts z!=0. Therefore a and b agree on every binary function constant in the Omega index. Applying all unary fiber indicators and left joint separation shows agreement on every Omega-valued function constant in that index.

Now let g:I times Omega->Omega be arbitrary. Its sections g_n(i)=g(i,n), viewed as functions constant in the Omega index, have common images. The Borel label l(i,n)=n also has a common image l. Source finite-operation identities yield, in the fixed sort of Z,

    [l=n] <= [a(g)=b(g)]       for every n.

Put d=0 where a(g)=b(g), and d=l+1 otherwise, using finite tuple operations. Every e_N d is zero by the finitely many corresponding displayed inequalities. Left separation gives d=0, and hence a(g)=b(g). The argument works in both sorts, proving a=b.

This replaces the countable-meet argument by a common Borel first-difference counter and left joint separation. It uses only M's monic cover of the finite family (v,f), so it remains valid even when Z has no global sigma-Boolean completion.
