# Every map from the antichain tail object to M kills its seed

2026-11-18. Status: PROOF_RECONSTRUCTED; independently reconstructed and checked by two research agents, not externally reviewed or formally verified. This is a ZFC obstruction to the proposed retract/amalgamation construction, not a Problem 4 counterexample. Section 6 strengthens the target hypothesis to a class preserved by all limits and directed monic unions.

Let W and x in W_1 be the tail object and binary antichain seed of [the antichain construction](omega_template_tail_antichain_obstruction_2026-11-18.md). Thus lambda=c^+, W is the tail quotient of copies of E(Y_omega), its second-sort rows are functions on M=End(Omega), and

    x_t = chi_(A_t),       A_t subset Sym(Omega),

where the nonempty A_t belong to distinct right-translation orbits. In particular W belongs to H_fg in ZFC.

**Theorem.** Let a:W->Z with Z in H_fg. If a_1(x) belongs to the image of a monomorphism j:E(Y)->Z, for any K-object Y, then a_1(x)=0.

Consequently every map W->Z with Z in M kills x. In particular W cannot embed into any M-object, and cannot be a retract of an M-object or an equalizer of maps whose source lies in M.

The proof uses a finitely generated test with only two generators. The many equations used below are encoded as unary equations inside that test; no finite-presentation assumption is made.

## 1. Packing c row functions into one generator

Suppose (f_i)_(i in I), |I|<=c, is a family of Omega-valued row functions on a common tail times M, all zero on finite-image transformations. Choose injections b_i:Omega->Omega with pairwise almost-disjoint infinite images. There is a row function z such that

    z·b_i=f_i       for all i.                           (1)

Indeed, put z(t,b_i r)=f_i(t,r) whenever r has infinite image, and put z equal to zero on all remaining transformations. Two such prescriptions from different i cannot conflict: a common infinite image would be contained in the finite intersection im(b_i) intersect im(b_j). For a fixed i, injectivity of b_i gives uniqueness of r. On finite-image r both sides of (1) are zero. Thus (1) holds pointwise, hence in W. The element z also vanishes on finite-image transformations.

There are c almost-disjoint infinite subsets of Omega, for example the sets of finite initial segments along branches of the binary tree, after identifying its countable node set with Omega.

## 2. Omitting one prescribed orbit pattern

Fix any p:M->{0,1} with p(id)=1. There is at most one t for which some r in A_t satisfies

    x_t(m r)=p(m)       for every m in M.                 (2)

If two distinct rows s,t admitted r_s,r_t, both r_s,r_t would be permutations, and substitution m=n r_t^(-1) would make x_s and x_t right translates, contrary to the antichain property.

Delete that possible row by passing to a later tail. For every remaining t and every r in A_t choose mu(t,r) in M such that

    x_t(mu(t,r) r) != p(mu(t,r)).                        (3)

Define row functions, extended by zero outside A_t, by

    C_n(t,r)=mu(t,r)(n),

    H_m(t,r)=0                         if mu(t,r)=m,
             1+min{n:mu(t,r)(n)!=m(n)} otherwise.

Also write X=x and X_m=x·m. All these functions vanish on finite-image r, including X_m.

For every m and N>=2, form a finite tuple code

    L_(m,N)=code(X,H_m,C_0,...,C_(N-2)),

and for every m form

    T_m=code(X,H_m,X_m).

Choose each tuple-coding bijection to send the all-zero tuple to zero. There are at most c functions in the resulting family, and all vanish on finite-image transformations. Section 1 gives one z in W_1 whose right translates include every X, X_m, C_n, H_m, L_(m,N), and T_m.

The following are unary equations among elements of the subpresheaf generated by z:

- Each coordinate projection of L_(m,N) and T_m is its displayed component.
- e_N H_m = phi_(m,N)(L_(m,N)), where e_N fixes 0,...,N-1 and sends larger integers to 0, and phi returns the first j+1 with j<N-1 and C_j!=m(j) when X=1, or zero if no such j exists or X!=1.
- psi_m(T_m)=0, where psi_m returns 1 exactly when X=1, H_m=0, and X_m=p(m), and otherwise returns 0.

The second equation is the definition of the first-difference counter, including its truncation. The third follows from (3). They hold pointwise on the chosen tail. The use of tuple codes is essential: all the finite-operation relations have become equations involving unary maps applied to actual right translates of z.

## 3. One element of E(Y) encodes all singleton comparisons

Suppose, towards a contradiction, that j:E(Y)->Z is monic and

    j_1(h)=a_1(x) != 0.

Applying a unary retraction onto {0,1}, and using monicity, shows that h is binary. The zero constants supplied by j and a agree: apply the constant-zero unary operation to j(h)=a(x). Since the image of h is nonzero, choose y in Y_1 with h(y)=1. Put

    v=chi_{y},       p(m)=h(m y).

Thus p is binary and p(id)=1; moreover j_1(v) is nonzero.

Fix a bijection c:Omega^2->Omega and its projections alpha,beta:Omega->Omega. Use these maps also as elements of M_1. Define

    d(z)=c(v(alpha z), h(beta z))       (z in Y_1).

For every m in M choose t_m:Omega->Omega by t_m(n)=c(n,m(n)). Then alpha t_m=id and beta t_m=m, so

    d·t_m = c(v,h·m).                                   (4)

Consequently a single element d has right translates which code all the pairs (v,h·m). The following unary equations hold in E(Y), hence in Z:

    pr_0(d·t_m)=v,
    pr_1(d·t_m)=h·m,
    k_(p(m))(d·t_m)=v,                                  (5)

where k_b(c(s,t))=s when t=b and is zero otherwise. The last equation holds because v is supported at y and h(m y)=p(m).

## 4. The two-generator contradiction

Apply Section 2 to the pattern p from Section 3 and obtain z in W_1. Let G be the ambient subpresheaf of Z generated by

    j_1(d) and a_1(z).

It contains j_1(v), all a_1(X_m), and all the tuple-code elements from Section 2. In particular the second projection in (5) is the same element as a_1(X_m). The displayed unary equations from Sections 2 and 3 are genuine equations of G.

Since Z belongs to H_fg, the inclusion G->Z factors as

    G --u--> E(Y') --b--> Z,       Y' in C.

The first map u is injective because its composite is the inclusion of G. Use primes for the resulting Omega-valued functions on Y'_1. The function v' is binary and nonzero, so choose y' with v'(y')=1.

Equations (5), which u preserves, imply

    X'_m(y')=p(m)       for every m,
    X'(y')=p(id)=1.                                    (6)

Define m_* in M by

    m_*(n)=C'_n(y').

For every N>=2, the coordinate equations for L_(m_*,N), together with the equation for e_N H_(m_*), imply

    e_N(H'_(m_*)(y'))=0.

The truncations (e_N) are point-separating on Omega, so H'_(m_*)(y')=0. The coordinate equations for T_(m_*) and the relation psi_(m_*)(T_(m_*))=0 now say that (6) cannot hold for m=m_*: their left side evaluates to 1, whereas their right side is 0. This contradiction proves the theorem.

The proof did not use any property of Y' beyond the fact that E(Y') is a pointwise full power. No N or NC input is used.

## 5. Consequences for the proposed envelope

If Z belongs to M and a:W->Z is any map, a_1(x) lies in a monic D-image by definition of M. The theorem therefore gives a_1(x)=0. Since x!=0 in W, no such map can be monic.

In particular the explicit sigma-Boolean amalgam in [the controlled intrinsic amalgam note](antichain_seed_controlled_intrinsic_amalgam_2026-11-18.md) is outside H_fg: it contains W monically and contains a monic normalized D-witness of the same nonzero x. Its satisfaction of the intrinsic FS-separation, finite tuple, left-separation, and E(sigma)-orthogonality conditions is consistent with this failure.

Thus the first intrinsic reflection preserves the chosen D embedding, but it necessarily leaves H_fg. Iterating that particular construction cannot establish an M-envelope of W. More strongly, no alternative construction can embed this W into an M-object at all. This rules out the antichain-tail W as a source of an actual M retract counterexample; it does not prove that M is closed under retracts or equalizers.

## 6. A limit-and-union invariant sufficient for the contradiction

Let L_full consist of the presheaves Z such that every ambient finitely generated subpresheaf G of Z admits a monomorphism

    G -> E(Y)

for some arbitrary K-object Y. No map back from E(Y) to Z is required, and Y need not belong to C. This property contains H_fg and is closed under:

- Subobjects: the same fg subpresheaf can be viewed in the larger object.
- Products: for G contained in a product of Z_i, its image G_i in each coordinate is fg. Embed G_i into E(Y_i); the induced map G->product_i E(Y_i)=E(coproduct_i Y_i) is monic.
- Directed monic unions: the finite generators of G all lie in one stage, and their generated subpresheaf there is exactly G.

Thus L_full is closed under all small limits and directed monic unions, and the least class A=LimUnion(D) is contained in L_full in ZFC.

The theorem actually holds for every target Z in L_full. Its proof does not use finite tuple operations on arbitrary elements of Z: all needed tuple codes are supplied separately by W and E(Y), and their relations are unary equations. In Section 4 replace the H_fg factorization by any monomorphism G->E(Y') supplied by L_full. This still preserves v!=0 and all the encoded equations, and the same point evaluation gives the contradiction. No return map E(Y')->Z was used.

Consequently the controlled intrinsic amalgam is outside L_full and hence outside A, not merely outside H_fg. More generally, no A-object can contain both the image of W with x nonzero and a monic full-power cover of that image of x.

This does **not** show that every W->A kills x: an arbitrary A-object has not been shown to supply a monic full-power cover of each element. The weaker local embedding property above supplies an embedding of a finite subpresheaf into a full power, with the opposite direction from the cover needed in Section 3. These two properties must not be conflated.
