# A small-profile criterion for simultaneous character extension

2026-11-18. Status: PROOF_RECONSTRUCTED; the coordinating research agent independently checked the complete argument and found no gap. Not externally reviewed or formally verified. This isolates a sufficient condition under which an uncountable family of E(sigma)-extensions can be handled at once. It does not establish that condition for the large ordinal sources.

Let Y belong to C, let a,b:E(Y)->Z with Z in M, and fix a finite seed on which a and b agree. The second sort of Y may be non-finite-support.

Suppose a componentwise first-sort quotient

    q_0:Y_0->Q_0

has the following properties:

1. |Q_0|<=c, it is finite-support, and E(Q_0) lies in the first-sort equality set of a,b.
2. It preserves the sort-0 seed and the p_Y- and q_Y-pullbacks of the full M_1-orbits of the finite sort-1 seed.
3. Its character subspace K=H(Q_0), embedded in H(Y_0) by q_0, is invariant under

       delta=H(q_Y) H(p_Y)^(-1).

The first-sort finite-support type-completion theorem supplies conditions 1–2 before any additional refinement; condition 3 is a substantive extra requirement.

For h in K write tilde_h:Y_1->Omega for its unique extension along p_Y. Define q_1:Y_1->Q_1 by the combined observation profile consisting of:

- all M_1-translates of the finite sort-1 seed;
- all tilde_h, for h in K.

No bound on the number of h is imposed; it can be as large as 2^c.

## 1. The full extension family gives an actual C-quotient

The defining equivalence of Q_1 is an M_1-congruence, since the seed observations include their full translates and each tilde_h is equivariant. Condition 2 makes the seed observations compatible with both structural arrows. For the extension observations,

    tilde_h p_Y = h q_0,
    tilde_h q_Y = delta(h) q_0.

Condition 3 therefore makes both arrows descend, giving an onto K-map q:Y->Q.

Every character of Q_0 extends to Q_1 by construction: tilde_h is constant on q_1-fibers and hence descends. Injectivity of H(p_Q) follows from Y∈C and the quotient map, as in the finite-support type-quotient note. Thus Q belongs to C.

Every defining observation of Q_1 is equalized by a,b. This is assumed for the seed and follows for its translates. For tilde_h, the two maps E(U)->Z induced by its unique extension agree after E(sigma), because the corresponding h q_0 is equalized in sort 0. E(sigma)-orthogonality of Z then gives agreement on tilde_h.

This is the exact use of the whole uncountable character family: it proves Q∈C without selecting only finitely many extensions.

## 2. Small profile diversity is enough; the family need not be packed

Assume in addition that |Q_1|<=c and that Q has packed countable separators. Then E(Q) lies in the equalizer of a,b and contains the chosen seed.

Indeed restrict a,b to E(Q). The strong packed-separator theorem factors both restricted maps through one monomorphism j:E(T)->Z, by actual K-maps

    tau_a,tau_b:T->Q.

There is no need for the packed separators themselves to be equalized: their images under both maps are simply included in the common finite monic cover used by that theorem.

In sort 0, agreement on all E(Q_0) gives tau_(a,0)=tau_(b,0). In sort 1, agreement on every defining profile observation gives equality of the two point images, since those observations separate Q_1 by definition. Thus tau_a=tau_b and the restricted maps agree everywhere.

If K is nonempty, Q has no fixed points in either sort, so the displayed cardinal bounds automatically give packed countable separators. If K is empty and Q_0 was a finite-seed type quotient, it has at most countably many fixed points; Q_1 is then also a finite-seed type quotient and has at most countably many fixed points. The fixed-point normalization/packing lemma again applies. More generally one may assume countably many fixed points in both sorts explicitly.

The crucial point is that the uncountable family need not fit into one ambient generated subpresheaf. Once its *realized profiles* form a small quotient, the two maps can be converted to actual dual point maps; those point maps can be compared on arbitrarily many coordinates.

## 3. A concrete sufficient bound using Baire profiles

The compact Stone space K=H(Q_0) has at most c clopen subsets: each clopen is determined by finitely many of the <=c finite-palette evaluation coordinates. Hence the sigma-algebra generated by its clopens has at most c members, and there are at most c Omega-valued functions measurable for that sigma-algebra.

Suppose every realized character profile

    K->Omega,       h |-> tilde_h(y),       y in Y_1,

is Baire-measurable, meaning measurable for the sigma-algebra generated by clopens. Then at most c such profiles occur. If the finite sort-1 seed's orbit-type quotient also has cardinality at most c, the combined quotient Q_1 has cardinality at most c.

This supplies the smallness hypothesis in Section 2 even when K itself and the family of its characters have size 2^c. Continuity of the profiles is a stronger sufficient condition, but is not required.

## 4. Why the ordinal boundary remains

For an ordinal source Y_kappa and a quotient whose character space contains the initial evaluation, delta-invariance and closedness can force the entire successor/limit orbit [0,kappa] into K. This is not automatic for a small first-sort quotient when kappa>c.

Even when Q_0 has size at most c, the non-finite-support second sort can realize arbitrary, non-Baire profiles on K. In the full ordinal second sort Omega^[0,kappa], these profiles need not form a set of size at most c. The Baire-profile condition is therefore not a consequence of C-membership.

The criterion separates two concrete missing inputs for the unrestricted equalizer problem: a small delta-invariant first-sort quotient preserving the seed, and a small set of realized second-sort profiles after all its character extensions are added. Neither is asserted in general.
