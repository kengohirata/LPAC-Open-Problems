# A controlled intrinsic amalgam for the antichain seed

2026-11-18. Status: PROOF_RECONSTRUCTED; independently checked by another research agent, not externally reviewed or formally verified. This constructs an actual split extension of the antichain tail object in the intrinsic finite-tuple/left-separation/FS-separation/E(sigma)-orthogonality class. The subsequent [omitted-pattern argument](antichain_seed_cannot_survive_in_m_targets_2026-11-18.md) proves that this extension is **outside H_fg**, and hence outside M. Thus it is not a retract counterexample for M or a Problem 4 counterexample.

Use the ZFC object W in [the antichain note](omega_template_tail_antichain_obstruction_2026-11-18.md), with lambda=c^+, R=Y_omega, and binary seed x in W_1. Write F for the ambient subpresheaf generated by x. In particular,

    p_W(x)=q_W(x)=0,       x·m=0 whenever m has finite image.

The first sort of F is precisely the constant Omega orbit.

## 1. A normalized D-witness with first sort Omega

Let I_fin be the left ideal of finite-image elements of M_1=End(Omega). For a sufficiently late tail T=[beta,lambda), form the pointed M_1-set

    Y_1 = the wedge, over t in T, of M_1/I_fin,

where all finite-image maps in each copy, and the resulting distinguished points from different copies, are identified to one point *. Put Y_0=1 and let both p_Y and q_Y select *. The point * is fixed by every m in M_1.

Then Y belongs to C in ZFC: Y_0 is finite-support, and both H_1(Y_0) and H_1(Y_1) are empty because Omega has no M_1-fixed point. The quotient maps

    T·R -> Y

are K-maps: the images of p_R and q_R consist of finite-image maps. Consequently D=E(Y) belongs to D, D_0=Omega, and there is a map g:D->W. The seed has the lift

    h(*)=0,       h(t,r)=x_t(r).

After choosing beta beyond the at most c relations defining F, this gives a map F->D whose composite with g is the inclusion F->W; hence F->D is monic. This normalization uses no N or NC.

Every d in D_1 satisfies the stronger identity

    d·m = const_(d(*))        whenever m has finite image.       (1)

In particular (1) holds for each standard truncation e_n.

## 2. Countably complete Boolean algebras and Omega-valued partitions

For a countably complete Boolean algebra A, let Gamma(A) be the set of countable partitions (a_k)_(k in Omega) of 1. A unary map m:Omega->Omega acts by merging partition cells along m. Finite pointwise Omega operations are defined by the corresponding countable joins of finite intersections.

For a set S there is a canonical identification

    Gamma(P(S)) = Omega^S.

Gamma preserves embeddings. Its objects have unique finite tuple coding and the joint-separation property for every point-separating family of unary maps on Omega. For joint separation, the fibers of a partition are recovered from the inverse-image partitions; a countable subfamily suffices because Omega has only countably many pairs of distinct points.

Each sort of W has this form. Its Boolean algebra B_i is the quotient of P(lambda times R_i) by the ideal of subsets supported on a bounded set of rows. The ideal is countably complete. Countable partitions in B_i lift to honest partitions after increasing the tail index, so Gamma(B_i) identifies with W_i. The two structural maps and the right M_1 actions preserve countable joins.

Let A be the smallest countably complete Boolean subalgebra of B_1 containing the binary fibers of F_1 and closed under the right M_1 action. Its size is at most c: starting with at most c generators, closure under countable operations and c unary actions for omega_1 stages still has cardinality c. Its first-sort counterpart is the two-element Boolean algebra, since p and q send its generators to constants.

The algebra A embeds simultaneously in B_1 and P(Y_1), after increasing beta in Section 1. Here is an explicit justification of the latter assertion. Represent every element of A by a countable-operation term in the translates of the binary row family x_t. Such terms are constant on every finite-image r, independently of t. There are at most c elements and at most c instances of the Boolean, countable-join, and M_1-action equations, since c^omega=c. Each equation holds on some tail. Regularity of lambda=c^+ gives one tail on which all these equations hold. The resulting term functions descend to Y_1 and define an injective homomorphism A->P(Y_1); injectivity follows because the composite into B_1 is the given inclusion.

Write epsilon:A->2 for evaluation on the collapsed finite-image region. Both p_W and q_W restrict to epsilon on A.

## 3. The Boolean amalgam preserves both embeddings

Form the pushout of countably complete Boolean algebras

    Q = B_1 coproduct_A P(Y_1).

The strong amalgamation theorem for countably complete Boolean algebras says that both canonical maps into Q are injective. We use the theorem in precisely this form. It is stated for general kappa-complete Boolean algebras in the proof of Theorem 3.2 of Ruiyuan Chen, *A universal characterization of standard Borel spaces*, Journal of Symbolic Logic 88 (2023), pp. 510–539, [published article](https://doi.org/10.1017/jsl.2022.85), p. 521, which attributes the result to R. Lagrange, *Amalgamation and epimorphisms in m complete Boolean algebras*, Algebra Universalis 4 (1974), pp. 277–279.

The right M_1 actions extend to Q by the pushout property. The maps p_W,q_W on B_1 and evaluation at * on P(Y_1) agree on A, so they induce two maps Q->B_0. Set

    Z_0=Gamma(B_0)=W_0,       Z_1=Gamma(Q).

This is an object of P. There are monomorphisms

    s:W -> Z,       j:D -> Z

which agree on F. Since g:D->W agrees with the identity of W on A, the pushout also gives a retraction r:Z->W with rs=id_W and rj=g. In sort 0, s and r are identities.

This is a concrete extension, not merely an existence claim about an unspecified localization.

## 4. No new balanced elements, hence E(sigma)-orthogonality

Call u in Z_i balanced when

    u·m = m·u       for every m in M_1=End(Omega),

using the identified copy M_2 on the left. The balanced elements of sort 0 parametrize maps X=E(B)->Z; the balanced elements of sort 1 parametrize maps E(U)->Z. These identifications use the finite tuple operations and the canonical constants. Under them, precomposition with E(sigma) is the map induced by p_Z.

For every u in Z_1 and every n,

    u·e_n = (s r u)·e_n.                                (2)

Indeed, the two countably complete Boolean homomorphisms expressing (2) agree on B_1, and they agree on P(Y_1) by (1), so they agree on its pushout Q. If u is balanced, then ru and sru are balanced as well, and (2) becomes

    e_n·u = e_n·sru       for every n.

The family (e_n) is point-separating on Omega. The left joint-separation property of Gamma(Q) therefore implies u=sru. Thus every balanced element of Z_1 already lies in s(W_1), and the converse is immediate.

Since Z_0=W_0, Z has exactly W's balanced elements in both sorts. W is orthogonal to E(sigma), so Z is also orthogonal to E(sigma). Its first-sort FS-separation property is inherited unchanged from W. Unique finite tuple coding and left joint separation were established in Section 2.

## 5. Consequence for the proposed intrinsic reflection

Let R_intr be the full class defined by unique finite tuple coding, left joint separation, first-sort FS-separation, and E(sigma)-orthogonality. Section 4 shows Z belongs to R_intr. It follows that, whenever the proposed reflector into R_intr is used, the canonical D-map into the reflection of the ordinary presheaf pushout

    W coproduct_F D

is monic: its composite with the induced map into the concrete object Z is the monomorphism j. The same holds for W, and the retraction onto W survives because W itself belongs to R_intr.

Thus, for this particular normalized antichain seed, the first intrinsic-reflection step does **not** collapse its D-witness. This resolves that specific obstruction to starting an envelope construction.

The subsequent [two-generator omitted-pattern proof](antichain_seed_cannot_survive_in_m_targets_2026-11-18.md) resolves the membership issue negatively: Z is outside H_fg. Indeed, that theorem excludes every H_fg target receiving a map from W which preserves x as a nonzero element of a monic E(Y)-image. Our Z has both embeddings and therefore satisfies exactly the forbidden configuration.

More strongly, every map W->M kills x, so W cannot embed into any M-object at all. Consequently the safe intrinsic reflection cannot be iterated into an M-envelope of this W. The construction remains a concrete example separating the listed intrinsic axioms from H_fg, and isolates why preservation of a D embedding under those axioms was insufficient.
