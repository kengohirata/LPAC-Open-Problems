# Open Problem 4 — Limits and directed unions versus reflectivity

## Problem

J. Adámek and J. Rosický, *Locally Presentable and Accessible Categories* (1994), Open Problems, p.296, Problem 4:

> Is every full subcategory of a locally presentable category which is closed under limits and directed unions reflective?

Directed unions must not be replaced by arbitrary directed colimits. The book's known general sufficient hypothesis is Weak Vopěnka's Principle (WVP).

## Current status — 2026-11-19

**At the first \(\omega\)-Erdős universe boundary, Problem 4 holds for every locally presentable ambient category.** With \(\theta=\kappa(\omega)\), the failure of a \(V_\theta\)-small solution set would give a sequence of \(\theta\) generated targets over one source with no marked map from an earlier target to a later one. Attaching the tree of finite descending sequences from each index to its partial-map tree gives a forbidden antichain by Eklof–Shelah, Theorem 6. If \(\theta\) exists, the first inaccessible universe and \(V_\theta\) thus give opposite answers while both satisfy (N). The interval from the first subtle cardinal up to, and excluding, \(\theta\) remains undecided by these results. [Endpoint proof and consequences for (N)](reflectivity_at_the_first_omega_erdos_universe.md)

**Problem 4 has a conditional counterexample when small sets belong to \(U=V_\kappa\) for the first uncountable strongly inaccessible \(\kappa\).** In a presheaf category on a finite category, a full replete subcategory is closed under all \(U\)-small limits and ordinary directed unions. The free presheaf on one root has no weak reflection. For each infinite cardinal \(\gamma<\kappa\), a small rooted model distinguishes the first \(\gamma\) rows specified by the root. Every rooted homomorphism preserves these rows, which rules out every proposed weak reflection by taking \(\gamma\) above its size. The actual one-root approximants consequently have sizes unbounded below \(\kappa\). The proof uses external ZFC to specify the \(U\)-large subcategory. Section 6 also gives an internally definable version in \(V=L\) with no strongly inaccessible cardinals. [Construction, closure proofs and universe conventions](nonreflectivity_in_the_first_inaccessible_universe.md)

More generally, the ordinal codes used in this construction exist exactly when there is no subtle cardinal at or below the universe boundary. Section 7 derives sufficiency from the binary-tree theorem of Adámek, Koubek, Nelson and Reiterman and proves necessity directly from subtlety. [Criterion for these codes](nonreflectivity_in_the_first_inaccessible_universe.md)

### Earlier results

**One actual comparison splits on every proper rooted subpresheaf of its earlier stage and has no global rooted map in the reverse direction.** In a presheaf category on a finite graph, use one free root and index the tests by infinite binary sequences. The stages at \(\mathfrak c=2^{\aleph_0}\) and \(2^{\mathfrak c}\) have precisely those cardinalities. Every countable fragment admits a section of the comparison over its inclusion. For every infinite \(\mu<\mathfrak c\), a single specified point needs exactly \(\mu^+\) candidate images over the fragments of size at most \(\mu\). These conclusions hold in ZFC even in the reflective subcategory generated by the two stage objects. [Objects, canonical stages, and local sections](countable_fragments_split_a_canonical_comparison.md)

**One fixed source and subcategory can exhibit \(\omega_1\) strict comparisons and require unbounded countable comparison depths.** The iterated construction still uses a finite graph and a single root. For each \(\xi<\omega_1\), the comparison from \(2^{\alpha}\) to \(\alpha=\beth_{1+\xi}\) splits on every fragment of size \(<\alpha\). For every countable ordinal there is a strict comparison that passes all positive tests up to that depth. Every individual strict comparison has a distinguishing test of countable depth. This tower first stabilizes at \(\beth_{\omega_1}\). [Transfinite construction and depth bounds](one_root_with_uncountably_many_locally_split_comparisons.md)

**A cardinal bound on the candidate images of each point suffices to glue local maps in ZFC.** Let \(M\) be generated by its marks in the subcategory and prescribe their images in \(N\). Give each point \(x\) an arbitrary list of at most \(\mu\) possible images in \(N\), where \(\mu^{<\lambda}=\mu\) and \(\mu\) bounds the signature and \(\lambda\). If every marked subpresheaf of \(M\) of size at most \(\mu\) has a map respecting these prescriptions, there is a unique such map on \(M\). The lists may differ at every point. For a fixed tower, every strict comparison after these small tests stabilize forces more than \(\mu\) different values at one point, however one chooses the local lifts. The values can all be taken in the same fiber of the eventual image over a chosen earlier stage. [Bounded ranges in reduced powers and the proof for actual comparisons](embeddability_of_reduced_powers_and_uniform_hulls.md)

**For a fixed source and tuple, ZFC reduces stabilization to separating pairs of constant functions in one reduced power per stage.** If the tower keeps changing, then for every prescribed size bound and earlier stage there are arbitrarily late stages with two distinct points in the eventual image over the same earlier point. Every fragment of the reduced power within the size bound containing their constants maps back to those original points. Every map from the whole reduced power to a subcategory object identifies them. Globally, a uniform bound on small generated subobjects is equivalent both to sufficiently directed union closure of the class of embeddable presheaves and to a uniform bound on how many premises any valid equational implication needs. For presheaves on any small groupoid the diagonal has a retraction and depth one suffices. The general pair-separation question remains open. [Fixed-source criterion, groupoid case, and equational implications](embeddability_of_reduced_powers_and_uniform_hulls.md)

**In ZFC, the fixed-tuple tower stabilizes exactly when its eventual images have uniformly bounded size.** The eventual images form an inverse system of pointwise surjections. If a tower still changes after all bounded-size lifting tests over a chosen earlier stage have stabilized, one point of the source of the lifting problem has two possible images on every fragment of the prescribed size. In contrast, maps into all algebraically compact targets stabilize simultaneously, even for a proper class of such targets. This gives a ZFC reflection theorem for classes generated by algebraically compact presheaves and a set of additional objects. For sets with a single unary operation, a single finite existential block detects every strict comparison for every finite tuple. [General theorems and the unary case](stable_images_compact_targets_and_comparison_depth.md)

**One finite existential block can miss a genuine comparison in a single canonical tower.** In a countable presheaf signature with one marked generator the approximant is countable below the continuum and stabilizes at the continuum. Every finitely generated marked fragment of the earlier object maps to the later one. A depth-two positive infinitary sentence distinguishes them, and every algebraically compact target identifies the two output values that split. The required depth for an unrestricted tower in ZFC remains open. [Explicit objects, closure, and exact depth](a_canonical_comparison_of_exact_depth_two.md)

**For a fixed locally presentable ambient category, a measurable cardinal above the size of a presentation suffices for a positive answer to Problem 4.** In a presheaf presentation of rank \(\lambda\), every subobject generated by fewer than \(\lambda\) marked elements has size \(<\Theta\) if \(\Theta\to(\omega)^{<\omega}_{2^\tau}\), where \(\tau=\max(\aleph_0,\lambda,|\mathrm{Mor}\,D|)\). A first \(\omega\)-Erdős cardinal above \(\tau\) also gives the bound. The proof constructs an actual morphism from consistently extendible partial maps by taking a directed union of products. A labeled tree compares whole blocks of fewer than \(\lambda\) elements in one move. In ZFC each point of a marked hull is individually definable there by a positive infinitary formula. A uniform bound on the necessary depth across the approximations for a fixed tuple remains open. [Proof, transfer to general ambients, and the ZFC criterion](partial_maps_partition_bound_and_reflectivity.md)

**In a locally finitely presentable ambient category, the closure of a set of objects under all small limits and directed unions is reflective in ZFC, for every source.** More generally the result holds in a locally \(\lambda\)-presentable ambient with \(\lambda\)-directed unions, and the closure is locally presentable. In the presheaf proof, maps to the original generators separate distinct elements of every subobject generated by fewer than \(\lambda\) marked elements. This gives a uniform cardinal bound and a solution set for every source. Every class in the general problem decomposes, at a suitable rank, as an increasing union of locally presentable reflective subcategories. Reflectivity of the whole class requires pointwise stabilization of their reflectors. [Set-sized generation and the stage decomposition](set_generated_limit_and_union_closures_are_reflective.md)

**Nonstabilization at a fixed source must be visible on one fixed small tuple of that source.** Under ordinary directed-union closure the tuple is finite. If the free presheaf on the tuple has a reflection, the corresponding generated subobjects form a coherent inverse system of strong quotients of that one reflection, with unbounded sizes and eventually the same kernel pair. After the kernel stabilizes, one effective quotient would force that entire tail of local subobjects to stabilize. Under N, general closure is equivalent to the explicit condition (EQ_C^epi).
- [Generic retract counterexample](mono_local_class_generic_retract_counterexample_2026-11-18.md): for a different full limit-closed D in a genuine presheaf category, the same monic finite-local construction fails retract and equalizer closure. The example uses the additive groups Z_2⊆Q_2 and a Q-linear retraction Q_2→Q. It also proves M⊊H_fg and failure of μ-filtered-colimit closure for every infinite regular μ in that example. This rules out a purely formal closure argument; it does not reject the actual Isbell candidate.
- [Fixed-template tail quotients](mono_candidate_tail_quotients_2026-11-18.md): long restriction-quotient chains built from copowers of any fixed Y∈C still have colimit in M once the indexing regular cardinal exceeds the row-pattern bound. The threshold depends on |Y|, so this does not prove uniform sufficiently-filtered-colimit closure.
- [Kernels as countably complete filters](mono_factorization_kernel_filters_2026-11-18.md): every component kernel of E(Y)→Z, Z∈H_fg, is equality modulo a countably complete filter. This describes possible quotient witnesses but does not turn them into D-subobjects.

### New closure theorems and excluded constructions

- [Coordinate stage uniformization](coordinate_stage_uniformization_by_pair_coloring_2026-11-18.md): if every scalar coordinate of E(Y)→V^R is a stage evaluation, all coordinates automatically have a common stage. A binary pair-coloring argument removes the formerly required bound on the number of target generators. In particular, |Y_i|≤c suffices in ZFC for **arbitrary** R.
- [Non-stage maps require an inner measurable](nonstage_scalar_maps_require_inner_measurable_2026-11-18.md): the scalar kernel is c^+-complete and its Boolean quotient has cellularity ≤c. A nonprincipal remainder would yield a saturated ideal and hence an inner model with a measurable cardinal, by Kunen's theorem as recorded by Kanamori–Shelah. Thus under **no inner model with a measurable cardinal**, in particular V=L, all E(Y)→V^R factor through a stage, with no cardinal restrictions. All M-equalizers into these V^R, and into subobjects of their products, are protected. This assumption is stronger than N.
- [Countable mono unions](countable_mono_unions_stage_factorization_2026-11-18.md): under N, all E(Y)→U factor through a stage when U is any countably cofinal mono D-union. Hence every M-equalizer with such a codomain, or a codomain embedding in a product of them, lies in M. Splitness is unnecessary. A split countable union additionally embeds into a D-product in ZFC.
- [A target outside all original V-template products](monic_local_tail_object_not_cogenerated_by_v_templates_2026-11-18.md): the c^+-tail quotient U of copies of Z_* is in M, yet has no map to any nonterminal original ω_1-length V^R, for **any** R. A ZFC Ulam-matrix argument proves this. Consequently the preceding V results do not cover all M-targets. [Long mono unions](long_union_obstructs_fixed_length_v_cogeneration_2026-11-18.md) give a separate conditional obstruction to using a fixed chain length.
- [Diagonal-tail equalizers](diagonal_tail_codomain_equalizers_and_row_lifts_2026-11-18.md): despite that cogeneration obstruction, every M-equalizer into a diagonal tail quotient with regular λ>c is in M, in ZFC; products and their subobjects are also protected. For arbitrary R∈C, E(Y)⇉U_R has equalizer in D whenever both |Y_i|≤c. The proof lifts small-index reduced-power maps to rows and coequalizes recurrent coordinate pairs.
- [Actual ordinal witnesses and diagonal targets](ordinal_witness_maps_to_v2_finite_coloring_2026-11-18.md): all E(Y_κ) maps to diagonal H_fg-targets factor through G=E(Z_*). In particular E(Y_κ)⇉V^(2) is excluded as an equalizer obstruction. [Countable p-isomorphism templates](ordinal_witness_maps_to_piso_templates_2026-11-18.md) and [Y_ω](ordinal_witness_maps_to_yomega_template_2026-11-18.md) are also excluded. - [Monic-p targets](ordinal_maps_to_targets_with_monic_p_2026-11-18.md): every E(Y_κ)→Z, for Z∈H_fg with monic structural p, factors through the countable Y_∞ in ZFC. For Z∈M this makes every such ordinal-source equalizer a D-object under N. No target cardinal bound or template presentation is required.
- [Exact minimal D-hull of j_0](ordinal_evaluation_has_no_proper_d_hull_2026-11-18.md): under N, a D-subobject of E(Y_κ) containing j_0 must be the whole object. A genuine non-stage map in the Y_(ω_1) reduction would therefore reject M. No such map has been constructed.

## 2026-11-17: H_fg candidate rejected

[(EQ_C) is incompatible with sufficiently filtered colimit closure](eqc_obstruction_sufficiently_filtered_colimits_2026-11-17.md).

The attempted general proof of (EQ_C) exposed a structural obstruction. Put c=2^aleph_0 and mu=c^+. Every ambient finitely generated presheaf is mu-presentable, so **H_fg is closed under all mu-filtered colimits, including diagrams whose maps are not monic**. If H_fg were equalizer-closed, its product closure would make it limit-closed. The published Reflection Theorem, or the corrected uncountable-orthogonality theorem of Hébert–Rosický (2001), would then make H_fg reflective. This contradicts its nonreflectivity under N and the adopted No.1 input (NC).

Consequently **N+(NC) implies that H_fg fails equalizer closure and (EQ_C) is false**. This is an existence proof of an equalizer obstruction, not an explicit formula for its codomain and arrows. The prior special cases, including all equalizers with codomain V, remain compatible with this result. V^(2) has not been identified as a witness.

The obstruction also applies to replacing the fg tests by any fixed set of test objects containing the obstructed source X. A P4 counterexample must fail closure under arbitrary mu-filtered colimits at every sufficiently large regular mu, while retaining directed-union closure. No P4 counterexample or general positive solution follows here.

Older entries below record earlier stages. Their statements that general H_fg equalizer closure is “unproved” are superseded by this conditional refutation. Statements below that NC is independently unaudited are also historical: the scoped independent agent audit linked above has since reconstructed its proof. The disputed No.3 density reduction is not used.

Positive investigation:

[Positive definability, hulls, and cardinal bounds](positive_definability_hulls_and_cardinal_bounds_2026-11-17.md).

### Conditional positive results

Definability here is the set-theoretic Lévy hierarchy with set parameters, in a fixed standard presheaf/structure representation of the ambient locally presentable category.

- A Σ1 object class closed under all small limits is reflective in ZFC.
- A Σ2 object class closed under all small limits is reflective assuming a proper class of **strong** cardinals. This means strong, not strongly compact.
- More generally, for Σ_n (n≥2), a proper class of Σ_n-strong cardinals, equivalently a proper class of Π_(n−1)-strong cardinals, is sufficient.
- These results apply Product Reflection to the complete comma category X↓A. They do not require directed-union closure. One strong κ only handles sources and parameters coded below κ; the proof does not use the same κ for all large sources.
- Using directed unions, a Σ2 class A needs only **one supercompact** above the fixed signature, definition parameters, and union rank, to obtain reflections of every source X, regardless of X's size. This is a fixed-instance localization, not a claim that supercompactness is weaker than WVP.
- The latter argument also yields strong-quotient smallness and hence local presentability of A, using the standard locally-generated theorem **with co-wellpoweredness included**.

### Main direct lemma

For B∈A, let cl_B(S) be the intersection of all ambient A-subobjects of B containing S. Limit closure makes this an A-object. Closure under λ-directed unions gives

    cl_B(S) = union {cl_B(T) : T⊆S, |T|<λ}.

A uniform set-sized upper bound on all <λ-generated hulls yields a solution set in every X↓A. The BCMR small-subobject theorem supplies such a bound for Σ2 classes under one supercompact.

This hull bound is not claimed equivalent to reflectivity. The [2026-11-18 continuation](positive_small_reflections_and_strong_quotients_2026-11-18.md) proves its equivalence to local presentability under the standing limit/union assumptions.

### Limits of the present result

The P4 hypotheses have not been shown to imply Σ2 definability, a set-sized axiomatization, or an accessible powerful-image presentation. Therefore none of the following is established for unrestricted P4:

    ZFC; a proper class of strong cardinals;
    a proper class of strongly compact cardinals;
    a proper class of almost strongly compact cardinals;
    a proper class of measurable cardinals.

The main note gives a limit- and directed-union-closed one-map orthogonality class that is not closed under θ-pure subobjects or θ-complete reduced powers, for each prescribed regular θ. The signature varies with θ. This rejects an unchecked fixed-rank compactness shortcut, not P4 and not all sufficiently large purity ranks.

### Dependencies of the positive investigation

The positive argument does not assume the proposed counterexamples or independence results in No.1–3. Its presheaf representation comes from the **ambient category's given local presentability**, not the disputed generated-generator-to-density claim audited in No.3. The negative investigation below has a different dependency: No.1's non-coreflectivity proof, now checked in the scoped independent agent audit linked above.
