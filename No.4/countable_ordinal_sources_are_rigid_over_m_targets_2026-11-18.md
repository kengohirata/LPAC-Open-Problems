# Countable ordinal sources are rigid over M-targets

2026-11-18. Status: PROOF_RECONSTRUCTED; the entire proof was independently
reconstructed by another agent with no gap found. See the scoped
[audit](independent_audit_countable_ordinal_rigidity_2026-11-18.md).
This is a direct ZFC safe-source theorem. It is not a proof of general
M-equalizer closure or a No.4 counterexample.

Use the standing P, E, C, D, H_fg and M. All targets below have the
finite pointwise tuple operations and left joint separation inherited
from their H_fg membership. Write e_N for the left unary map fixing
0,...,N−1 and sending all other integers to 0.

## 1. Countable packing on a fixed-point-free M_1-set

Let S be an M_1-set with no fixed point. Choose injections b_n:ω→ω
with pairwise disjoint images, and left inverses a_n. The maps
b_n:S→S are injective. Their images are pairwise disjoint.

Indeed, if b_n s=b_m t=u for n≠m, for each k choose a transformation
r which is the identity on im(b_n) and constant k on im(b_m). Then
u=r u=c_k t for every k. Thus every transformation fixes u. Applying
a_n shows that s is fixed too, contrary to the hypothesis.

Consequently, for any countable family f_n:S→ω there is z:S→ω such
that z(b_n s)=f_n(s). Set z arbitrarily off the disjoint union. In E(S),

    z·b_n=f_n.

The same construction packs countably many finite tuple codes, by
regarding the codes themselves as the functions f_n.

## 2. Agreement on packed countable separators

**Lemma.** Let a,b:E(Y)→Z with Z∈M, and fix a sort i. Suppose a
countable family f_n:Y_i→ω separates the points of Y_i and is
contained in the ambient subpresheaf generated by finitely many
elements z_1,...,z_r of E(Y). If a(f_n)=b(f_n) for every n, then
a_i=b_i.

In particular the packing hypothesis holds if Y_i has no M_1-fixed
points. No cardinal hypothesis on the other sort is used.

**Proof.** Suppose a(F)≠b(F) for some F:Y_i→ω. By M-membership
choose one mono j:E(Q)↪Z, Q∈C, whose image contains all a(z_l),
b(z_l),a(F),b(F). It therefore contains all common f_n-images.
It is closed under the finite pointwise operations, because those
operations are uniquely specified by their tuple projections.

Let F_a,F_b and s_n be the corresponding functions on Q_i. The
binary function [F_a≠F_b] is nonzero. Choose q∈Q_i where it is 1,
put w=j(χ_{q})≠0, and set p_n=s_n(q). Then

    w≤[a(F)≠b(F)],       w≤[a(f_n)=p_n]=[b(f_n)=p_n] for all n. (1)

Define the first-difference function C_p:Y_i→ω by

    C_p(y)=0                         if f_n(y)=p_n for every n,
           1+min{n:f_n(y)≠p_n}       otherwise.

For every N≥2, e_N C_p is a finite pointwise function of f_0,...,f_(N−2).
Thus (1), finite operations and preservation of those operations by a
imply that every left truncation of the conditional element

    d = if w=1 then a(C_p) else 0

is zero. Left joint separation gives d=0. Hence w≤a([C_p=0]).
The same argument gives w≤b([C_p=0]).

If p is not the profile of a point of Y_i, [C_p=0]=0, contradicting
w≠0. Otherwise, because the f_n separate points, there is a unique y
with that profile and [C_p=0]=χ_y. The pointwise inequalities

    χ_y≤[F=F(y)]

then give w≤[a(F)=F(y)] and w≤[b(F)=F(y)], contradicting (1).
This proves the lemma. □

Notice that C_p is chosen after q. It need not lie in the selected
D-subobject. Its images a(C_p),b(C_p) are available in Z, and finite
tuple operations and left separation in Z suffice for the conditional
argument. No closure under arbitrary or countable meets in Z is assumed.

## 3. Agreement passes through countable ordinal limits

Let κ be any ordinal and put

    Y_κ=(C([0,κ]) →→ ω^[0,κ]),

with inclusion and successor precomposition as usual. Let a,b:E(Y_κ)→Z,
Z∈H_fg. For β≤κ, denote the first-sort evaluation on C([0,κ]) by
u_β and the second-sort evaluation on all functions by v_β.

**Lemma.** If β is a limit of a strictly increasing sequence β_n and
a(u_(β_n))=b(u_(β_n)) for all n, then a(u_β)=b(u_β).

**Proof.** Every g∈C([0,κ]) is constant on a sufficiently late part
of that sequence, with value g(β). Define N(g) to be the least n such
that g(β_m)=g(β) for all m≥n. This is an ω-valued function on the
first-sort carrier; it need not be continuous there.

The M_1-action on C([0,κ]) has no fixed point, since [0,κ] is nonempty
and postcomposition by different constant transformations gives
different functions. By §1, pack the functions

    code(N,u_(β_n),u_β)      (n∈ω)

into one element z of E(Y_κ)_0. Let G be the ambient subpresheaf of Z
generated by a(z),b(z). It contains all displayed components and tuple
codes from both maps. In G the code projections are genuine unary
equations, as are

    N≤n ⇒ u_(β_n)=u_β,

expressed using a unary test of each tuple code. The common
u_(β_n)-images are equal as elements of G.

Factor G↪Z through E(Q), Q∈C, using H_fg. The first map G→E(Q)
is mono because its composite is the inclusion. At any point of Q_0,
choose n at least both lifted values of N. The two lifted u_(β_n)
values are equal, so the unary tuple equations force equality of the
two lifted u_β values. Thus their functions are equal, and monicity
of G→E(Q) reflects their equality back to Z. □

This step requires only H_fg. The two-generator packing makes all the
countably many tuple equations visible in one finitely generated test.

## 4. Consequence for every countable ordinal

**Theorem (ZFC).** Let κ be countable. If a,b:E(Y_κ)→Z with Z∈M
agree on j_0:X→E(Y_κ), then a=b.

First a(u_0)=b(u_0). The elements v_β are balanced. Since Z is
E(σ)-orthogonal, equality of a(u_β),b(u_β) forces equality of the
unique balanced lifts a(v_β),b(v_β). Applying q then gives equality
of a(u_(β+1)),b(u_(β+1)), whenever β<κ. At every nonzero countable
limit, §3 gives equality. Transfinite induction therefore proves
agreement on all u_β and v_β for β≤κ.

For each sort, this is a countable point-separating family on the
carrier of Y_κ. Neither carrier has an M_1-fixed point. The packing
lemma and §2 imply equality of a,b on each whole sort.

No N hypothesis is used in this implication. For uncountable κ the
countable-limit step still proves agreement through every countable
ordinal below κ, but it does not handle limits of uncountable
cofinality or provide countable separators for the full second sort.
The theorem therefore leaves the genuinely uncountable ordinal tests
open.
