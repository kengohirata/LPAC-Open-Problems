# No.16 — Vopěnka's principle versus weak Vopěnka's principle

**Status: already solved in the literature. This repository contains no new work on this problem.**
This note only records the references. It was compiled with an AI assistant (see the top-level README).

## The problem

Adámek–Rosický, *Locally Presentable and Accessible Categories* (1994), Open Problems, p. 297, Problem 16:

1. Is Vopěnka's principle (VP) equivalent to weak Vopěnka's principle (WVP)?
2. Is the existence of arbitrarily large measurable cardinals equivalent to WVP?

WVP was introduced by Adámek, Rosický and Trnková (1988), who showed that VP implies WVP.
The book shows that WVP implies the existence of arbitrarily large measurable cardinals.
So both questions ask whether the reverse implication holds.

## Answer: no to both (Wilson)

- **Question 1.** T. M. Wilson, *Weak Vopěnka's Principle does not imply Vopěnka's Principle*,
  Advances in Mathematics (2020). [doi:10.1016/j.aim.2020.106986](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2020.106986),
  [arXiv:1909.09333](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09333).
  It proves that WVP is equivalent to semi-weak Vopěnka's principle (Thm 1.4).
  It also proves that if κ is the first supercompact cardinal, then V_{κ+1} satisfies semi-weak Vopěnka's principle
  and "there is no supercompact cardinal" (Thm 1.2). Since VP implies that supercompact cardinals exist, WVP does not imply VP
  (assuming a supercompact cardinal is consistent).
  The paper attributes the question to Adámek–Rosický–Trnková (1988). It does not refer to Problem 16 of the book by number.
- **Question 2.** T. M. Wilson, *The large cardinal strength of Weak Vopěnka's Principle*,
  [arXiv:1907.00284](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00284).
  The paper shows that WVP is equivalent to the large cardinal principle "Ord is Woodin".
  That principle is strictly stronger in consistency strength than the existence of arbitrarily large measurable cardinals.
  So the latter does not imply WVP.

Follow-up work: J. Bagaria and T. M. Wilson, *The weak Vopěnka principle for definable classes of structures*,
Journal of Symbolic Logic (2023), [arXiv:2012.11202](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.11202).
